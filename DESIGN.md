# Design System: OhMyShot

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Part of macOS Apple Forgot to Ship"**

OhMyShot looks like a system feature, not an app with a brand. Light and dark follow the system, the accent is the user's own macOS accent, and every window is a standard window with standard controls. What the app adds is a thin, consistent layer of softness for the things that float over other people's screens: continuous-corner cards, fully round capsules, gentle downward shadows, opaque white action capsules, and the rounded system face with fixed-width digits for every live reading. The capture is the hero; the chrome recedes.

Density is low and the vocabulary is small. A handful of shapes (12 pt cards, capsules, one circular loupe) and a handful of tints (black and white at stated opacities over system colors) cover every shipped surface: the capture overlay, the All-In-One bar, in-place annotation, the Self-Timer countdown, Pick Color and its color card, the Measure ruler, screen recording (its bar, control bar, keystrokes, camera bubble, and window), Quick Access cards, Capture History, the OCR panel, the annotation editor, and Settings. Light and dark are first-class and are resolved per view, and Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, and Increase Contrast each have a shipped, specific response rather than a generic fallback.

The confirmed rejection is the first world, "Contact Sheet" (rejected by the user on 2026-10-07): no frame numbers, no captures joined into one strip, no film-edge or darkroom motifs, no dark heavy panels, nothing that reads as a Linux utility.

Units: every dimension in this file is in points. The frontmatter writes points as `px` for tool compatibility; 1 pt renders as 2 device pixels on Retina displays.

**Key Characteristics:**
- System semantic colors and the user's accent carry all appearance; literal values are only black or white at fixed opacities.
- Continuous corners everywhere: 12 pt cards, fully round capsules for every control and reading.
- Soft, downward, widely blurred shadows; never a hard edge or a halo.
- SF Pro Rounded with monospaced digits for readings; plain system text styles inside windows.
- Liquid Glass only on small controls (the All-In-One bar, the in-place toolbar, the editor's tool rail, the Self-Timer disc, History hover buttons, window toolbars), never over a full-screen frame.
- Motion is a single short ease-out or nothing; under Reduce Motion it is nothing.

## Colors

A system-colored world: macOS semantic colors and the user's accent do the work, and the app's own values are neutral black and white at stated opacities.

### Primary
- **The User's Accent** (`AccentColor`, AppKit `controlAccentColor`): the only hue the app ever uses for state. It marks the selected mode's symbol in the All-In-One bar, the tool in hand in the editor's rail and in the in-place toolbar, the in-place toolbar's Copy check, the selected variant in the style capsule, the ring around the chosen color dot, the editor's selection box and handle rings, the center cell outline in the magnified loupe (Pick Color's and the actual-size lens's choose white or black against the sampled pixel instead, over an outline of the other), the Measure ruler's lines and box edge, the dashed outlines of the redaction tool's suggestions, and the 3 pt selection ring around a selected Capture History card. The ruler's box carries a wash of the accent at 0.08 with no text on it (the box reading stands outside the box). It is never a fill behind text and never a brand color; OhMyShot's chrome has no brand hue (the brand mark's fixed hues stay in the mark; see Brand Mark under Components).

### Neutral
- **System surfaces** (`windowBackgroundColor`, `controlBackgroundColor`, SwiftUI `.background.secondary`): the opaque backing inside the All-In-One and in-place toolbar glass, the Reduce Transparency capsule, the letterbox behind a Quick Access picture, and the placeholder behind a History thumbnail. These follow light and dark automatically; no hex is recorded because none exists.
- **System ink** (`labelColor`, `.secondary`, `.tertiary`, `separatorColor`): all window text, bar labels, the empty-state symbol (tertiary), a disabled Undo or Redo in the in-place toolbar (tertiary), the bar's hairline edge, and History card borders.
- **White** (#FFFFFF): the 1.5 pt selection border and the thick corners of a resizable frame, the crosshair, the loupe rim and fill, and the loupe's 1.5 pt center cell over dark sampled pixels while picking a color or in the actual-size lens (black over light pixels). White reads on the dimmed screen; the dark outline underneath makes it read on white pages.
- **Selection Outline** (black at 0.32, 0.7 with Increase Contrast): a 3.5 pt stroke under the white edge and the crosshair (the picker's center cell uses a 3 pt opposite-color outline at the same opacity), so the white line holds on light content.
- **Ruler Outline** (white at 0.85, 3 pt; solid white at 4 pt under Increase Contrast): the Selection Outline's device turned over for the ruler. A 3 pt white stroke under each 1 pt accent line and the box edge (2 pt over 4 pt under Increase Contrast), so the accent holds on any content, the accent's own color included. The code calls it a halo; it is an outline under a line, never a glow.
- **Suggestion Outline** (white at 0.85, 1.5 pt; solid white at 2 pt under Increase Contrast): the white line under a redaction suggestion's 1.5 pt accent dashes (2 pt under Increase Contrast), as wide as the dashes, so the gaps between them are white and the outline holds on any content, blue link text and dark pages included. It is the Ruler Outline's device at the line's own width instead of 3 pt: suggestion outlines sit tight around text and side by side in dense lines, and a 3 pt white line reached onto the glyphs and made neighboring outlines touch (checked on a dark page). There is no fill inside: the accent is never a fill behind text, and this is the text being judged.
- **Room Dimming** (black at 0.26, 0.45 with Increase Contrast): the flat fill over the screen outside the selection, in the capture overlay and in in-place annotation, and over the display around a recorded area when Settings › Recording › Recorded area dims the rest of the screen. Light enough that the screen stays readable.
- **Recording Frame** (the system red, 3 pt): the frame around a recorded area when Settings › Recording › Recorded area is Red frame, lying wholly outside the area, so it frames exactly what is recorded and is never in it.
- **Reading Capsule** (white at 0.96 in Light; a near-neutral 0.17 gray at 0.94 in Dark): the fill of every on-screen reading, with ink at 0.85 black or 0.92 white and a 0.5 pt border at 0.08 black or 0.12 white.
- **Action White** (#F7F7F7, #FFFFFF on hover, #D6D6D6 pressed, ink #1A1A1A): the opaque Copy and Save capsules over a hovered Quick Access card. They stay white in both appearances because they sit on a picture, not on chrome.
- **Corner Button** (black at 0.5, 0.62 on hover, 0.72 pressed, white symbol): the small round Close and More buttons on a hovered card.
- **Scrims** (black at 0.22 over floating Quick Access cards; black at 0.28 over in-window History cards): the hover dimming that lets white or glass controls read on any picture.
- **Picture Border** (black at 0.10 in Light, white at 0.16 in Dark, 0.5 pt): the edge of a Quick Access picture.
- **Saved green** (`systemGreen` with a white glyph): the saved badge on a card, the one non-accent hue, used only as a status mark.
- **Chip Fill steps** (selected black 0.08 / white 0.16, hover black 0.045 / white 0.09; with Increase Contrast selected black 0.16 / white 0.28, hover black 0.09 / white 0.16): one pair for every tinted chip in a floating control. The editor's rail and style capsule carry the Increase Contrast steps; the All-In-One bar uses the base steps.
- **Capsule Surface** (white 0.97; Dark a 0.17 gray at 0.96): the fill of the text result card, a text pin's card, and the editor's style capsule, the reading capsule's materials at control and card size. The style capsule draws its Dark fill as a pure 0.17 gray where the text card lifts blue to 0.18; the difference is 1/255 and is not a second token.
- **Contrast Hairline** (black 0.35 / white 0.5, 1 pt): the edge every floating capsule, card, and picture takes under Increase Contrast in place of its 0.5 pt hairline (reading capsules, the text result card, the lens, the in-place toolbar, the editor's rail, capsule, and picture).
- **Spotlight Dimming** (black at 0.5): what a spotlight mark leaves of the picture outside its spots; overlapping spots stay bright. It is drawn into the picture, so it is darker than the room dimming, which only shows a selection.
- **Capsule Swatch Edge** (black 0.18 in Light, white 0.38 in Dark, 0.5 pt; 1 pt at black 0.40 / white 0.60 under Increase Contrast): the edge of every color dot and background swatch in the style capsule, so white and yellow hold on the light capsule and black and Graphite on the dark one.
- **Checkerboard** (8 pt squares in view points; Light white and a 0.91 gray, Dark a 0.20 and a 0.25 gray; 0.84 and 0.30 under Increase Contrast): what shows through a Transparent background's room on screen, and under a translucent color pin's swatch (there too 8 pt on screen at any zoom). It is an on-screen aid and is never exported.

### Mark Inks
Colors a user puts into a picture are content, not chrome, and are fixed sRGB values so the screen and the export agree in either appearance. The style capsule offers eight, in this order: **Red** (#FF3B30, the default for every tool), **Orange** (#FF9500), **Yellow** (#FFCC00), **Green** (#34C759), **Blue** (#007AFF), **Purple** (#AF52DE), **Black** (#000000, the default for solid redaction), and **White** (#FFFFFF); the color panel adds any other opaque color. **Marker Yellow** (#FFE74A, multiplied into the picture) is the highlighter's default. Ink on a mark (text on a label, a counter's number, the outline around outlined text) is black 0.88 on light colors and white on dark ones, chosen by the color's luminance.

### Background Fills
A background is content too, fixed values so the screen and the export agree. The ten gradients are a corner-to-corner blend (top-left to bottom-right) with one or two soft radial glows over it, in the manner of a desktop picture: **Sky** (#9CC8FA to #D9C6FA, the default), **Lilac** (#A996DC to #F6C5E6), **Rose** (#FFA6A9 to #FDD6EC), **Peach** (#FFE3C2 to #FFA488), **Citrus** (#F9DB72 to #FCA982), **Mint** (#CFF5DE to #5ACFC2), **Ocean** (#5E9CF5 to #37CDBE), **Dusk** (#464FBF to #C07BE8), **Graphite** (#4A4E57 to #1E2024), and **Paper** (#FBFBFC to #E6E7EC). Dusk and Graphite are the dark ones. Besides them: **Transparent** (no fill; only the card's shadow is drawn), **Blurred Capture** (the capture blurred until only its colors are left, aspect-filled), a **Background Color** of one's own (opaque, from the color panel without alpha), and a **Picture** (aspect-filled, read no larger than it shows). A missing picture falls back to Sky; a blur not yet made, to Paper.
- **Matte:** canvas reaching past the capture (combined images beside it), and the room Inset and Auto Balance add, is filled with the capture's edge color, or white where the edge has no one color, on screen and in exports. A window captured with its shadow gets no matte; its surroundings stay clear.

### Named Rules
**The No Brand Hue Rule.** The accent is whatever the user chose in System Settings. Never hard-code a blue in chrome, never tint a surface toward a house color. Settings' sidebar tiles are not a house color: they are system colors, one per pane, as System Settings has them. Mark inks and background fills are the user's content and the one place fixed hues live. The one exception is the brand mark: the app icon's ground and pixel inks, and the wordmark's O, are the one place the brand carries fixed hues. Chrome still never takes them, and the menu bar glyph takes no hue.

**The Black-and-White Opacity Rule.** Every literal color the app's chrome introduces is pure black or white at a stated opacity (or the single 0.17 gray of the Dark reading capsule, and the opaque grays of the on-screen checkerboard). New surfaces reuse these steps before inventing a tint.

**The Resolve-Per-Appearance Rule.** Layer colors do not follow appearance changes on their own; every layer-drawn color is resolved for the view's effective appearance and re-resolved when it changes.

## Typography

**Display Font:** none; OhMyShot has no display tier.
**Body Font:** SF Pro (the system face) via SwiftUI and AppKit text styles.
**Label/Mono Font:** SF Pro Rounded (system font with the rounded design), with monospaced digits for readings.

**Character:** The rounded face softens the few words that float over someone else's screen; the plain system face keeps windows indistinguishable from Apple's own.

### Hierarchy
- **Reading** (SF Pro Rounded semibold 600, 12 pt, monospaced digits): live numbers on screen, such as "1200 × 720", "App · W × H", and "x, y  #HEX". Fixed-width figures keep the capsule from jittering as values change. The Self-Timer disc's seconds take the same face at 26 pt.
- **Control Label** (SF Pro Rounded medium 500, 12 pt; semibold 600 when selected): All-In-One bar labels. Symbols beside them are 13 pt medium.
- **Action Label** (SF Pro Rounded semibold 600, 13 pt; symbol 12 pt bold): the Copy and Save capsules on a card.
- **Title** (system `title3` semibold, 15 pt): empty-state titles in Capture History; `title2` semibold for the About and onboarding headings.
- **Body** (system body, 13 pt): Settings rows and window text.
- **Callout** (system `callout`, 12 pt; medium for a History card's relative time): secondary explanations and empty-state messages, max width 360 pt; regular in secondary ink for the style capsule's "Finding text…" and "Couldn't read text" statuses.
- **Caption** (system `caption`, 10 pt, secondary): a History card's "W × H · App" line; semibold with monospaced digits in the recording badge ("GIF" in place of the play mark for a GIF).
- **Capsule Text Chip** (system semibold 600, 12 pt, label color): the words in the editor's style capsule (crop's Reset and Done, the Redact tool's Redact All). The capsule sits inside a window, so it takes the system face.
- **Mark Text** (system semibold 600, 14 / 20 / 28 / 40 pt steps, 20 by default, 1.25 line height): text the user types into a picture, drawn by the same layout on screen, in exports, and in the editing field, so nothing moves when editing ends.
- **Counter Label** (SF Pro Rounded bold, half the disc's diameter, shrunk to fit 72% of it): the numbers, letters, or Roman numerals in counter marks.
- **Card Readings** (the rounded face, monospaced digits): on the color card, the title at 15 pt semibold (13 pt when longer than 12 characters, so "color(display-p3 …)" fits), each notation's value at 12 pt medium, and the contrast figures ("3.9:1", "Lc 64") at 13 pt semibold. They are readings, so they take the rounded face under the Rounded-Off-Window Rule; a floating card's prose (the text result card, a text pin) stays in the system face.
- **Field Label** (SF Pro Rounded medium 500, 11 pt, secondary ink): the color card's row names (HEX, RGB, HSL, OKLCH, Display P3, Background, WCAG 2, APCA), its subtitle, and its contrast verdicts.
- The editor's zoom menu label ("100%") uses monospaced digits.

### Named Rules
**The Rounded-Off-Window Rule.** The rounded face is for floating controls and readings over the screen. Inside a standard window, use system text styles and never the rounded face. A counter's rounded numerals are content drawn into the picture, not chrome.

**The Steady Digits Rule.** Any number that changes while the user watches uses monospaced digits.

## Layout

There is no page grid; layout is anchored to screens, selections, and windows.

- **Overlay:** the size capsule centers under the selection 10 pt below it; with no room below it tucks 10 pt inside the selection; if it would meet the All-In-One bar it lifts 10 pt above the bar. All floating readings clamp 6 pt from the display edges. The loupe sits 22 pt below-right of the pointer and flips away from the display edges; its info capsule sits 8 pt below the lens. With Settings' actual-size magnifier, a screenshot's or Annotate Area's lens is centered on the pointer instead, its info capsule 8 pt below it, flipping above and clamping inside the display at edges. The All-In-One bar centers horizontally 40 pt above the bottom of the display and fades out while a region is dragged.
- **Pick Color and Measure:** the Copied reading sits 14 pt below and right of the point, where the magnifier's reading was, flipped left or up 6 pt from the visible frame. The ruler's width reading sits 8 pt above the middle of its line across (below near the top), its height reading 8 pt right of the middle of its line down (left near the right edge); the box reading centers 10 pt below the box, above it without room; all clamp 6 pt from the display's edges. The color card centers under the point, 6 pt below a 24 pt square around it, above without room, and 8 pt inside the visible frame.
- **In-place annotation:** the toolbar stands 10 pt below the selection, right-aligned to it, and the style capsule 6 pt under the toolbar, right-aligned to the toolbar. When that stack (42 + 6 + 38 pt) does not fit below, both go above, the capsule above the toolbar; when it fits neither way, the toolbar sits inside the selection's bottom-right, 10 pt clear of the frame as drawn, with the capsule above it, right-aligned to the toolbar. Everything stays 8 pt inside the display. The side is chosen with the capsule's band reserved whether it shows or not, so picking a tool never moves the toolbar. The size reading sits 10 pt above the selection's top-left corner; covered or off screen, 8 pt inside that corner (when the selection has 16 pt to spare); too small for that, 8 pt left of the toolbar on its row; else 10 pt below the selection. It keeps 6 pt from the toolbar and capsule and clamps 6 pt from the display's edges.
- **Quick Access:** a single column of separate cards, 220 pt wide (Settings › Quick Access: Small 180, Medium 220, Large 260), each 100 to 172 pt tall by the capture's aspect (extreme shapes letterbox; 100 to 141 on Small, 100 to 203 on Large, the 172 scaled with the width). Newest is at the bottom; the oldest card drops when the column would exceed the visible height. The column stands on the display the capture was taken on; with "Show on the display with the pointer" on, on the display the pointer is on when the card appears (a pointer on no display leaves it where the capture was taken). A column holds as many as the card is tall enough for, each 8 pt clear of the bottom: two under the corner button on a 100 pt card, three at 130, four at 172; a card too short for all leaves the last ones to its menu, which has every action. A recording's left column stops 24 pt higher, above its length badge at the bottom-left. Trash is never the button beside Close. The saved badge stands 8 pt from the bottom-right and hides while the pointer is over the card.
- **Capture History:** a standard 920 × 640 pt window (minimum 560 × 420) with an adaptive grid of cards 200 to 280 pt wide, 20 pt between columns, 22 pt between rows, 24 pt side insets, 20 pt top and bottom.
- **Annotation editor:** a standard window sized to show the capture at its on-screen size plus 100 pt across and 138 pt down (at least 920 × 660, at most 90% of the visible screen; minimum 910 × 640, the width of the widest style capsule, the Redact capsule while a reading has failed and with a full row of kept colors (838 pt), beside the rail, and the height of the rail over the capsule's band under the toolbar's 52 pt). The picture is fitted, never above on-screen size, inside canvas insets of 24 top, 72 left (the rail), 62 bottom (the capsule's band), and 28 right, sized on whole points. The tool rail stands 12 pt from the left edge, centered in the height above the 62 pt band and never closer than 12 pt to the top. The style capsule centers 10 pt below the selected marks, goes 10 pt above them without room, and drops to the window's bottom when the selection fills the view; it clamps 8 pt from the edges and never left of the rail plus 8 pt. Beside a highlight snapped to text it also keeps clear of the capture's lines of text, 6 pt to spare, where 10 pt below would cover the next line to highlight: past the lines under the highlight to the first gap that holds it, or past the lines over it, whichever is nearer (below on a tie), and where it would otherwise go when neither fits. With nothing selected it rests in the band at the rail's foot, 12 pt from the left and the bottom. With a background the fitted picture is the whole output, background and all; crop fits the plain capture again.

Rows are 12 pt apart (8 pt for the shortcut recorders, whose labels center on them; elsewhere a label sits on its controls' first baseline). Groups of rows are split by a divider with 4 pt above and below, never by headers or footers. The rows start at the top of the pane; a pane taller than the window scrolls. A pane is made the first time it is chosen. Non-form panes (About) use 28 pt padding.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is soft shadow plus system materials. Floating elements over arbitrary screen content carry a diffuse downward shadow and a 0.5 pt hairline so they hold their edge on white pages; in-window elements carry a lighter shadow. Shadows always follow the element's exact rounded shape. Liquid Glass gives the All-In-One bar its rim, but its fill is opaque, so screen text never shows through a control.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Floating card** (black 0.22, blur radius 14, offset 6 pt downward): Quick Access cards, the text result card, and the color card.
- **Floating lens** (black 0.28, blur radius 12, offset 4 pt downward): the loupe.
- **Floating bar** (black 0.16, blur radius 10, offset 3 pt downward): the All-In-One capsule, the in-place toolbar, and the Self-Timer disc.
- **Floating reading** (black 0.18, blur radius 8, offset 2 pt downward): reading capsules, the in-place size reading, the ruler's readings, and the Copied reading among them.
- **Action lift** (black 0.18, blur radius 4, offset 1 pt downward): the white Copy and Save capsules, so they hold on bright pictures.
- **In-window card** (black 0.12, blur radius 10, offset 4 pt downward): Capture History cards, the OCR panel's picture, and the annotation editor's picture.
- **Mark shadow** (black 0.3, blur 3 pt, drop 1 pt, scaled with the capture): drawn into the picture under arrows, lines, shapes, pen strokes, text, and counters (on by default; off for highlighter, redaction, and spotlight; off by default for combined images), so marks hold on busy pictures. It is exported.
- **Background card** (for Shadow strength s from 0 to 1: drop 2 + 10s pt, blur 8 + 32s pt, black at 0.14 + 0.30s; at the default 0.5, drop 7, blur 24, black 0.29; scaled with the capture): under the capture on a background, following the card's rounded shape, drawn from a small blurred mask so a large picture's shadow costs milliseconds, and never seen through clear room in the card. It is exported. A window captured with its shadow keeps its own and gets none.

The editor's tool rail takes the floating bar shadow and its style capsule the floating reading shadow; selection handles carry a small lift (black 0.3, blur 2, 1 pt down) of their own. The Adjust popover is the system's popover with its own shadow.

`Theme.Shadow` also declares a 5 pt offset that no shipped surface uses; the card applies 6 pt.


A floating panel leaves room around its card for the whole shadow to fade out: twice the blur plus the drop (34 pt for the floating card shadow; the Self-Timer disc's panel leaves 24 pt for the floating bar shadow). A shadow cut off at the panel's edge reads as a hard rectangle.

### Named Rules
**The Soft Lift Rule.** Shadows are downward, widely blurred, and low opacity. No hard offset shadows, no glows, no halos. A thin overlay line's contrast outline (the dark stroke under the white selection edge and crosshair, the white stroke under the ruler's accent lines and under a redaction suggestion's dashes) is part of the line, not a halo.

**The Small Glass Rule.** Liquid Glass appears only on small controls: the All-In-One bar, the in-place toolbar, the editor's tool rail, the Self-Timer disc, window toolbars and their glassProminent Copy, and History hover buttons. The two overlay toolbars are small controls on the overlay, not over it; the full-screen overlay itself is a flat dimming fill and never carries glass, and neither does the editor's style capsule, which sits beside text-bearing marks.

**The Opaque Behind Text Rule.** Any control with text that floats over a capture or a screen has an opaque (or at least 0.94) fill. Glass supplies the rim, not the backing; the editor's Adjust popover sets its Form on the opaque window color inside the system popover.

**The Exported-Is-Drawn Rule.** What is drawn into the picture is exported: marks, the mark shadow, the background, its card shadow, and the matte. What helps on screen never is: the checkerboard, selection and handles, the drop ring, the crop frame and the in-place selection frame, redaction suggestions, the picture border, and the in-window shadow.

## Shapes

Continuous (squircle) corners throughout. Pictures and cards are 12 pt, except the annotation editor's picture, which is square: it is the image that gets exported, edge to edge, and marks and the crop run to its pixel edges (on a background, the capture's card inside it takes the background's Corners, 0 to 40 pt, 12 by default); every control, reading, and badge is a full capsule whose radius is half its height; nested controls are capsules concentric with their capsule container (the bar insets its chips by 6 pt). The History selection ring is 16 pt (card radius plus 4), drawn 5 pt outside the card so the corner stays concentric. The loupe is a circle with a 3 pt white rim; its center pixel cell has a 1.5 pt corner. The color swatch in a reading is a 10 pt square with a 5 pt radius. The color card's swatch is 28 pt with a 7 pt continuous corner, its contrast swatch 16 pt with a 4 pt corner, both with the capsule swatch edge. The ruler's lines are square-capped and end in 8 pt caps across them; its box is square-cornered, on whole pixels. The editor's rail is a capsule 42 pt wide whose 30 pt chips are circles; the style capsule is 38 pt tall with 30 pt chips 4 pt inside it, concentric; the in-place toolbar is the rail laid flat, a capsule 42 pt tall with 30 pt circular chips 6 pt inside it. The drag-out thumbnail in the editor's toolbar has a 4 pt continuous corner. A pin, picture, text card, or color swatch alike, takes Settings' pin Corners (0 to 24 pt, 12 by default; 0 is square), continuous, and its focus ring follows the same corner. Background swatches are 14 pt discs in 24 pt chips; the Position grid's cells are 16 × 12 pt with 3 pt continuous corners. A redaction suggestion's outline is a rectangle with 3 pt corners around its cover, drawn in view points so it keeps its size at any zoom.

Hairlines are 0.5 pt (card borders, bar, rail, and in-place toolbar edges, reading and style capsule edges, picture edges); the capture, crop, and in-place selection edge is 1.5 pt; the editor's mark selection is a 1 pt accent line; Increase Contrast turns the hairlines into the 1 pt contrast hairline and adds a 1 pt outline to card buttons.

### Reading Capsule (Signature)
The rounded capsule for every on-screen number: the selection size, the window name and size, the loupe's coordinates with color swatch and the color in Settings' notation, hex by default (what a click or C copies), the ruler's lengths and box size, a pin's zoom and opacity (and its Copied and Locked), and the Copied reading. The swatch border is strong enough (black 0.3 / white 0.38) that a white sample still reads. ### Upload Badge (Quick Access)

While uploading: a 13 pt ring in the badge's ink (never the accent, which can be a color that vanishes on white) filling clockwise from twelve o'clock, the percent in the reading face (never above 99% until the server has answered), and a small cancel mark that takes a click. ### Quick Access Keyboard and Closing
How a card is driven without the pointer, closes by itself, and changes its picture. The panel is borderless and non-activating, as a pin's is.

- **Keyboard:** the panel takes the keys only when asked, and then without bringing the app forward, so the app in front stays in front: by a click on a card's picture (not a drag, not a button, on release) or by the Control Quick Access shortcut, which has no keys until chosen (Settings › Shortcuts) and gives the newest card the keyboard. Hovering, dragging, and a card appearing never take it. The card that has the keyboard wears a 1.5 pt ring in `keyboardFocusIndicatorColor` just inside its edge, over the hairline, as a pin does, and is held from closing by itself. The cards answer their own keys, since an accessory app has no menu bar to route them: ⌘C Copy (the capsule says "Copied"), ⌘S Save (Show in Finder once saved), ⇧⌘S Save As…, ⌘E Annotate (Edit or Play for a recording), ⌘O Open, Space Quick Look, ⇧⌘T Recognize Text, ⇧⌘P Pin to Screen, ⌘P Print…, ⇧⌘U Upload to the default destination, ⌘L and ⌘R rotate, Delete or ⌘Delete Move to Trash, Escape or ⌘W Close, ⌥⌘S Save All, ⌥⌘W Close All. The arrows move the ring between cards: ↑ and ← to an older card, ↓ and → to a newer one, stopping at the ends. A key with no meaning beeps. The menu shows the same keys beside its items.
- **Handing the keyboard back:** Escape, ⌘W, Close All, and the actions that bring a window forward (Annotate, Open, Recognize Text, Print, Save As) take the ring away and hand the keys back to the app in front: the panel is ordered out and in again, which returns them without hiding the cards. Moving the card that has the keyboard to the Trash keeps the keyboard, on the card beside it; trashing another card, with its button, leaves the ring where it is. A click in another window takes the keys as it always does, and the ring goes with them.
- **Quick Look:** Space, or Quick Look in a card's menu or VoiceOver actions (which first give that card the keyboard), opens the system's preview panel on the capture, with the system's Live Text on a picture and playback for a recording. It stays up while another app is in front. The arrows in the preview move between cards and the preview follows (so does a picture changed under it); Space or Escape closes it, and the keys come back to the card. The card is held from closing by itself while it shows.
- **Closing by itself:** off by default. Settings › Quick Access sets a time of 3, 5, 10, 20, 30, or 60 seconds from the card's appearance, and what happens then: Close, which leaves the capture in Capture History, or Save and Close, which saves a copy to the export folder first unless the card was saved, and keeps the card if the copy cannot be saved (with history off, closing would delete the capture). The copy is named from the file name pattern and never asked for (Ask for a name does not apply: nobody is at the card, and an alert raised by a timer, one for each card, would take the keyboard from the app in front); a copy already being saved from the card is the one waited for, so a card is saved once. With History set to Never, Close takes a capture that was not saved with its card, as closing it by hand does: the pane then shows a warning line under the pickers (an orange `exclamationmark.triangle.fill` and "History is off: a card that closes unsaved takes its capture with it." in callout, secondary ink), and the help button says the same. A capture something else holds is not taken: one open in the editor, one whose upload goes on, a recording with a recording window or a Studio window open, or a recording whose file the clipboard still holds (copied when it was made, or since from its card, Capture History, or its window); a recording is let go when its window closes or the clipboard holds something else, which is looked at every 3 seconds. Time stops while the pointer is over the card, its menu is open, it is being dragged out, Quick Look shows it, it has the keyboard, the annotation editor has its capture open (the card is not saved or closed under an editor that is about to put its marks over the picture), or its upload goes on or failed and waits on Retry; when the last of these ends the card has at least 4 seconds left (its whole time, if that is shorter), so a card never goes the moment the pointer leaves it or its upload finishes. A card brought back from Recently Closed starts its time again. Nothing runs while no card counts down: a single timer sleeps until the first card is due.
- **Save All, Close All:** at the foot of the menu while there is more than one card. Save All saves every card not yet saved, one after another, as Save does on each but under the file name pattern without asking for a name, being one command for many files (an alert for each, which Escape on one would not stop, is not what it is for); Close All closes them all, each as Close does, so Recently Closed brings them back one at a time. A card that is being saved when it is asked to save again (a second ⌘S, Save All over a Save, a card closing by itself) waits for that save instead of making another, so a capture is one file and takes one number.
- **Transform ▸** (screenshots): Rotate Left (⌘L), Rotate Right (⌘R), Flip Horizontal, Flip Vertical, and Scale to 1×, which brings a Retina capture down to one pixel per point (dimmed, "Already one pixel per point.", once there). The new picture takes the capture's place: its file and its history record take the new picture, size, and, after Scale to 1×, a scale of 1 (so pins, printing, Recognize Text, and the editor, which opens it at 100%, show it at its size), with no original or marks kept beside it, so the editor, restoring from Capture History, and Revert all see the changed picture. Turning and flipping move whole pixels and blend none, and the picture is written again as a PNG in its own color space and depth (8 or 16 bits to a channel), so they lose nothing (Scale to 1× resamples, high quality); a picture that is no PNG (a photo added from a JPEG or HEIC file, which would lose some of itself being written again, and whose camera orientation is not in its pixels) and a recording have no Transform. The card shows the new picture, reshaped (a card that was saved offers Save again, the saved copy being of the picture as it was), the History thumbnail follows, and the text in the picture is read again. A capture that has an original beside its picture (marked up, cropped, or put on a background) is not changed, and its marks do not turn with it: the items are dimmed with the tooltip "This capture has marks or a background. They would stay as they were."; one open in the editor says "Close the capture’s editor first." Changes made in a row run one after another, each as the capture then is (Scale to 1× chosen twice before the first is done scales once), and their pictures come onto the card and into History in that order.
- **VoiceOver:** a card's actions add Quick Look, Rotate Left, Rotate Right, Flip Horizontal, Flip Vertical, and Scale to 1× (each only while it can be done), and with more than one card Save All and Close All.

### Translation Overlay
How translated text is drawn over an image, in the OCR panel and Translate Area alike: each translated line is painted over with its own background (the median of the pixels just outside its box, 12% of its height above and below, never into a line that stays), then the translation is set in the original's ink (the median of its inkiest pixels), in the system font for the target language (PingFang for Chinese), its ink left-aligned with the ink of its column (translated lines and lines left as they were alike) and centered on where the original's ink was; right to left, it ends at the right edge. Its size is the original's, read from the line's width at the size it had on screen (a formula in the line measured as it is drawn readable, not as its LaTeX), made one across neighbors of a column or row; a paragraph takes its size from its lines of words alone where it has some; a unit a third larger than the body or more is set semibold. A translation that does not fit shrinks to 70% (a line on its own may first run on to the right over its own background, never over an icon or other text), then ends in an ellipsis; a paragraph keeps its column and line spacing. Code, links, figures ("5 GB"), bilingual labels, a formula set apart, and a table's rows are left as they are. A formula among text is drawn readable rather than as its LaTeX ("θₜ₊₁=θₜ−η∇L(θₜ)", "½xᵀAx", in the translation's font), or as its LaTeX, whole, when it holds something that can't be written so (a matrix); the translated text shown and copied keeps the LaTeX.

### Pins
Things kept above every app's windows for reference while working, in three kinds: a picture (a capture, or a picture from the clipboard or a file), a text card, or a color swatch (both from the clipboard). Each pin is its own window holding its own copy of what it shows, so a captured picture outlives the capture's place in the history. A picture's text can be selected in place with Live Text. What follows holds for every kind unless it names one.

- **Panel:** borderless and clear, non-activating, at the floating level, on every Space and over full-screen apps, left out of the window cycle, with no window animation. It takes the keys only when clicked, without bringing the app forward (Escape, the arrows, and ⌘C reach it while the app in front stays in front); a new pin never takes them.
- **Picture:** the image at its own size in points (zoom 100%), continuous corners from Settings (0 to 24 pt, 12 by default), the system window shadow as the Translation Lens has (Settings › Shadow; it lies outside the window, so nothing around a pin catches the pointer), and a 0.5 pt black 0.18 hairline (Settings › Border), 1 pt black 0.35 under Increase Contrast, where it is always shown. While the pin has the keys, a 1.5 pt ring in `keyboardFocusIndicatorColor` follows its corner inside the edge, most of it over the hairline; a locked pin shows none.
- **Text card:** the Text Result Card's materials: the capsule surface (white 0.97; Dark a 0.17 gray, blue lifted to 0.18, at 0.96) and the reading capsule's hairline (0.5 pt black 0.08 / white 0.12; 1 pt black 0.35 / white 0.5 under Increase Contrast). The hairline follows Settings › Border as a picture's does, always shown under Increase Contrast; corners, shadow, and ring are a picture's. The text is in label ink, 11 pt from the top and bottom and 14 pt from the sides, in the system face at 13 pt, or the monospaced system face at 12 pt for code: a fenced block, or two or more lines indented by a tab or at least two spaces (read conservatively, since a false positive sets a sentence in a monospaced face). The card is as wide as its text, 64 to 420 pt, and as tall as its lines, at most 30; the rest is cut with an ellipsis, trailing blank lines are dropped, and no more than 4000 characters are ever laid out, while Copy still takes the whole text. The text is laid out once at 100 percent and drawn scaled with the zoom, so it wraps in the same places at any zoom and stays sharp. It selects as text does, in the system's selection color: the cursor over its lines is an I-beam, a drag on them selects, a double click takes a word and a triple click a paragraph. A drag anywhere else moves the card: in the padding, past a line's end (a few points of slack), within 6 pt of the edge, or with ⌘ held, where the cursor is the arrow. A press on the card away from its text clears a selection; one by the border or with ⌘ held keeps it. Right-clicking the text shows the text's own menu (Copy, Look Up, Translate, Share, and the like), then the pin's, where Copy All Text takes the whole text.
- **Color swatch:** a 128 × 128 pt square of the color as it came, in its own color space (a Display P3 color shows as itself; its hex and rgb are its sRGB value, clamped). A translucent color sits over the checkerboard, 8 pt squares on screen at any zoom in the checkerboard token's grays, so it never seems more solid than it is. Corners, shadow, ring, and the 0.5 pt black 0.18 hairline are a picture's.
- **Hover controls:** Close (top-left) and More (top-right), 24 pt card corner buttons 8 pt in from the top corners, fading in while the pointer is over an unlocked pin. On a pin too short to leave 8 pt above them (under 40 pt tall, such as a one-line text card) they center in its height, so they never jump as it zooms. Close needs a pin at least 40 × 36 pt, and More stands beside it on one at least 72 pt wide: a narrow pin has Close alone, a tiny one neither. On a text card they stand on the card's own fill, so they cover the ends of a line cleanly instead of showing its letters through the disc. Faded out they take no clicks, so the pin drags from under them. More opens the pin's menu just below itself.
- **Reading:** a reading capsule centered 10 pt above the bottom edge (centered in the height on a pin too short for that) shows the zoom ("150%"), the opacity ("Opacity 60%"), "Copied", or "Locked" as they happen; the text lands at once, stays 0.9 s, then fades over 0.12 s. A color pin's hex ("#3366FF"; "#3366FF80" when translucent) rests there from the start, while the swatch has room for it (at least the capsule plus 16 pt in width and in height; with less it hides); a passing reading shows whatever the size and, after its 0.9 s, gives way to the hex instead of fading.
- **Lock mark:** a locked pin wears a card corner button disc with `lock.fill` where More would be (centered in a short pin's height, as the buttons are), for as long as it is locked.
- **Placement:** Capture Area and Pin puts the pin over the captured area at 1:1, so nothing seems to move until it is dragged (a window captured with its shadow is centered on its frame). Any other pin is centered on the pointer's display, scaled down to fit 80 percent of its visible area when larger; one that would land exactly on another steps 24 pt right and down until it is clear.
- **Pointer:** drag anywhere to move, except on a picture's text (Live Text, below) or a text card's lines, where a drag selects. Scroll or pinch zooms around the pointer, 10 to 800 percent (about 10 percent a wheel line); a trackpad's momentum after the fingers lift is ignored, and each step takes its size from the pin's own size times the zoom, so zooming in and out never bends it out of shape. Option-scroll sets the opacity, 20 to 100 percent (5 percent a wheel line), fading the picture (with any selection in its text), the text card (fill, hairline, and text), or the swatch only: the reading, buttons, ring, and lock mark stay solid, so the reading that says how faint it is can still be read. A middle click closes it.
- **Live Text** (pictures): VisionKit reads the picture's text and codes once, the first time the pointer comes to the pin (or the pin opens under the pointer, or takes the keys): about 0.25 s, up to 0.5 s for the first read in a session. No locales are named, so the preferred languages lead. Then, as in Preview, the cursor over text is an I-beam and a drag selects, in the system's selection color, clipped to the pin's corners and faded with the picture. A drag anywhere else moves the pin, as does one within 6 pt of the edge even over text (so a picture filled with text still drags by its border) or one with ⌘ held; the cursor there is the arrow. Links, addresses, phone numbers, and dates in the text are detected as Live Text detects them; a QR code takes only a right press, for its menu, so a drag across it moves the pin. A press on the picture away from its text clears a selection; one by the border or with ⌘ held keeps it. Right-clicking text shows Live Text's menu (Copy, Look Up, Translate, Share, and the like) without VisionKit's image items, then the pin's, where the picture's copy reads Copy Image (⌘C only while nothing is selected). VisionKit's own Live Text button stays hidden. One analyzer serves every pin and goes with the last; while pins are open each read holds about 20 MB, and Vision's models (about 30 MB) stay loaded after the last pin closes, as after the OCR panel.
- **Keys:** ⌘= and ⌘− zoom by 1.25 around the center, ⌘0 is actual size; the arrows move it 1 pt, 10 pt with Shift; Escape closes it, first clearing a selection in a picture's text or a card's, and ⌘W closes it; ⌘C copies that selection when there is one, else the picture, the text, or the hex (the reading says "Copied"; a card's text selected whole copies all of it, the part past the card's cut too); ⌘A selects all of a picture's text once it is read, or all of a card's; ⌘S saves, ⇧⌘S saves as, and ⌘P prints, for pictures only. The pin answers its own ⌘ keys, since an accessory app has no menu bar to route them.
- **Menu** (right-click or More): first what the kind offers (a picture: Copy ⌘C, or Copy Text ⌘C and Copy Image (`photo`) while its text has a selection, then Select All Text ⌘A (`character.cursor.ibeam`) once its text is read, Save ⌘S, Save As… ⇧⌘S, Print… ⌘P (`printer`); Annotate…, Recognize Text…; a text card: Copy Text ⌘C, or Copy ⌘C and Copy All Text while part of it is selected, then Select All Text ⌘A; a color: Copy Hex ⌘C (`number`) and Copy RGB (`eyedropper`; "rgb(51, 102, 255)", or "rgba(51, 102, 255, 0.5)" when translucent)), then, for every kind, Zoom ▸ (Actual Size ⌘0, Zoom In ⌘=, Zoom Out ⌘−), Opacity ▸ (100, 80, 60, 40, 20%, the current one checked), Lock; Close, Close All Pins. Top-level items carry symbols as the card's menu does; the submenus' items and Close All Pins have none.
- **Lock:** a locked pin lets every click through to what is under it and cannot take the keys, so it is unlocked from the status menu (Unlock Pins); Lock's tooltip says so. The reading says "Locked" once as it locks.
- **Reached by:** Capture Area and Pin (kept in the history, without a Quick Access card or the clipboard: the pin is where it went), Pin to Screen in a Quick Access card's menu or in Capture History, and Pin from Clipboard or Pin Image… in the status menu. Pinning while pins are hidden shows them again. Pin from Clipboard takes, in order: a picture (a picture file, or picture data; data that cannot be read beeps); then a beep for a copied file that is not a picture, rather than pinning its name; then a copied color; then text that is exactly a color's value (#RGB, #RGBA, #RRGGBB, #RRGGBBAA, rgb(), or rgba(), surrounding space allowed; a bare number is not a color), as a color pin; then any other text that is not blank, as a text card; otherwise a beep. Settings' Pins footer reads "Pin a capture from its Quick Access card or Capture History; the clipboard's picture, color, or text, or a picture file, from the menu bar; or an area with Capture Area and Pin."
- **In captures:** with "Show pins in new screenshots" on (the default), pins are in new captures as they are on screen; off, or while hidden, they stay out like the rest of OhMyShot.
- **VoiceOver:** "Pinned." is announced as a pin appears. Every pin has the value "N percent, opacity N percent" and ends its actions with Lock and Close. A picture reads as an image, "Pin, W by H points", with Copy, Save, Annotate, and Recognize Text before them; once its text is read, "Pin with text, W by H points", with Copy Text after Copy; a text card as static text, "Text pin: " and the text it shows, with Copy; a color as an image, "Color pin, #3366FF", with Copy Hex and Copy RGB. A card's text is no element of its own, so with VoiceOver Copy takes all of it, not a part.

### Annotation Editor
A standard titled window named for the capture, unified toolbar, full-size content, on `.background.secondary`. The capture owns it: tools stand in a slim glass rail at its left edge and a mark's style sits in a capsule beside the mark; there is no wide tool toolbar and no fixed option strip. Reached by double-clicking a Quick Access card, Annotate… in a card's menu, Annotate… on a screenshot in Capture History, Annotate Image… in the status menu (a picture is added to Capture History first), Open in Editor in in-place annotation (the marks stay editable), Capture Area and Annotate, Annotate Latest Capture (the newest screenshot in Capture History, whether or not its card is still up, though with history set to Never only a capture something still shows is there; a beep when there is none), Open from Clipboard (the clipboard's picture, added to Capture History first, as the URL API's `open-from-clipboard` adds it, named from the file name pattern as a capture is and taken at the resolution it carries as a file from Open With is; a beep when the clipboard holds none, and the status menu entry dimmed unless its types offer a picture or a file), or a project (.ohmyshot) opened from Finder; one editor per capture. Capture Area and Annotate, Annotate Latest Capture, and Open from Clipboard have shortcuts, with no keys until chosen. An editor opened from a project shows the project's proxy icon in the title bar. Edits are kept in the capture itself when the window closes and whenever the capture leaves it (copied, saved, dragged out): its file then shows the marks, the marks stay editable, and the card and History thumbnail show the new picture.

- **Toolbar:** Undo and Redo at the leading edge (disabled when there is nothing to undo); then, trailing, the Picture menu (`crop.rotate`, tooltip "Resize, turn, or flip the picture"; Image Size… ⌥⌘I, a divider, Rotate Left ⌘L, Rotate Right ⌘R, Flip Horizontal, Flip Vertical, a divider, and Lock Canvas, checked while on), the zoom menu (its label the current percentage, monospaced digits; Zoom to Fit ⌘0, Actual Size, Zoom In ⌘+, Zoom Out ⌘−), a 34 × 24 pt drag-out thumbnail of the picture as it is now (aspect-fill, 0.5 pt black 0.18 edge, open-hand cursor; what is dragged out is the picture in the format Settings choose), Save, a fixed space, and one `.glassProminent` Copy with title and symbol (⌘C). Save is a split button: its face saves a copy to the export folder (tooltip "Save a Copy (⌘S)"); its menu holds Save a Copy as Project… (⇧⌘S), which keeps the capture and its marks, still editable, in one .ohmyshot file. Copy and Save swap to "Copied" / "Saved" with a checkmark for 1.5 s; VoiceOver hears "Copied" or "Saved". A failure beeps. Print… (File, ⌘P) is not on the toolbar: it prints the capture as it looks, marks and background and all, the print panel as a sheet on the window, and keeps nothing.
- **Picture commands:** the Picture menu's commands are also the Image menu's, which follows Edit (the main menu is never shown, but its keys work); the editor in front answers them, they are off elsewhere, and off in place, where the canvas is the selection. A turn or flip carries the whole picture: the capture, the canvas (a crop included), and every mark. Text and counters stay upright, moved to where their middles go; arrows keep their curve (a flip swaps its bend); combined images turn with it. The background stays around the picture, its shape and position as set. One undo step each, named "Rotate Left", "Rotate Right", "Flip Horizontal", "Flip Vertical", "Resize Image"; VoiceOver hears that name, or for a resize "Resized to W by H pixels". A crop being chosen is made first, in the same step (one Undo takes back both). A picture that can't be made, such as one too large for the memory there is, changes nothing and beeps. Edits stay editable: the capture's file and the history keep the capture as it was taken, and the document keeps the turns and the size beside the marks (a project the same), so the picture is made from the two whenever it is drawn, exported, or opened again; a document that was never turned is written as it always was, and one written before turns read as it was.
- **Image Size:** a sheet on the window (340 pt wide, a grouped form on the window color): Width and Height in pixels, Scale in percent (up to two decimals), each a right-aligned field with monospaced digits and its unit in secondary ink, and Keep proportions on by default (changing one side moves the other; Scale always scales both). It is asked of the canvas, so a cropped picture is resized to the size it is kept at, and a crop being chosen is measured by its frame but made only by Resize (Cancel leaves it as it was, the crop still in hand), in one undo step with the resize: the footnote reads "Now W × H px. The marks scale with the picture." and, past what a picture can be (16384 px a side, 100 million in all), "A picture can't be this large." in red, with Resize off. Return in a field commits it and resizes; Escape cancels. Marks scale with the picture, and so does the scale their point sizes (strokes, type, corners, shadows) are drawn at, so they keep their proportions to it; text grows from its corner and a wrapped block's width with it. Actual Size and Print follow that scale, so they keep the size the picture had on screen: a picture halved is the same picture at half the pixels per point. The capture's scale in Capture History follows when the edit is kept, so a 2× capture resized to half is 1× for everything that reads it (saving at 1×, a drag out, Copy, an upload, a pin, printing, Recognize Text), and 2× again once the resize is undone and the capture put back; a capture from before scales were kept keeps none.
- **Picture:** square-cornered, the picture border (0.5 pt; the contrast hairline under Increase Contrast) and the in-window card shadow; both are hidden for a window captured with its shadow, whose corners are transparent, and on a Transparent background, where only the card's own shadow marks the edge, so no rectangle is drawn that is not in the picture. With a background, border and shadow wrap the whole output.

### Style Capsule (Signature)

It uses the capsule surface (white 0.97; Dark 0.17 gray at 0.96), a 0.5 pt hairline (black 0.08 / white 0.12; the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast), and the floating reading shadow; 38 pt tall, 4 pt inset (8 pt at the leading edge), chips 2 pt apart, sections split by 18 pt separator hairlines with 6 pt around them. No glass.

- **Color dots:** 24 pt chips holding a 12 pt disc with the capsule swatch edge (black 0.18 / white 0.38, 0.5 pt; 1 pt black 0.40 / white 0.60 under Increase Contrast), so white and yellow hold on the light capsule and black on the dark. The chosen color is ringed in the accent (2 pt) instead of filled. Other Color… shows a hue wheel, or the custom color ringed when one is in use, and opens the system color panel without alpha.

A full row widens a capsule by 8 × 26 pt, which with the Redact capsule's failed reading is what the editor's minimum width is for.

The Shadow chip draws a rounded square over its own soft shadow.
- **State:** selected chips take the selected chip fill and an accent-tinted glyph; hover the hover fill; Increase Contrast the contrast fills. VoiceOver reads chips by name with "Selected", and Shadow as On or Off.
- **Crop:** the capsule holds the ratio chip, a divider, and Reset and Done, text chips in the capsule text face (title width plus 28 pt). The ratio chip is titled with the shape in use and a "▾" ("Free ▾", "Original ▾", "16:9 ▾"; tooltip "Crop Ratio", VoiceOver "Crop Ratio, 16:9"); it opens a menu below or above it, with the shape in use checked: Free, Original (the picture's own shape), a divider, 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9, 9:16. Done carries the selected fill with label-colored text: the accent is never a fill behind text.
- **Combined images:** a selected image shows only Shadow and Delete.
- **Redaction suggestions:** with the Redact tool, a last section after Delete (kept when a redaction drawn by hand is selected, since the rest still wait). While the capture is being read it holds a quiet status where the chip will come: a small system spinner and "Finding text…" in 12 pt regular secondary ink, 6 pt apart, 8 pt leading and 12 pt trailing inset, 30 pt tall, read by VoiceOver as text. Once read, it holds "Redact All (N)", a plain text chip in the capsule text face (not the prominent fill), which covers every open suggestion in the current redaction method as one undo step named "Redact All". With none open the section is absent. When the capture could not be read (neither reading made, as when Vision runs past its time), it holds "Couldn't read text" in the status face without the spinner (4 pt trailing inset) and a plain "Try Again" text chip that reads it again; VoiceOver hears "Couldn't read text" where it would hear the count.
- **Highlighter reading:** with the Highlighter, while the capture is being read for its lines of text, the same status closes the capsule, after the sizes; it leaves once the lines are in, or gives way to Couldn't read text and Try Again, as for suggestions, when they could not be read. A selected highlight snapped to text shows its colors and Delete, no sizes.

### Canvas
- **Selection:** a 1 pt accent box (2 pt under Increase Contrast) with 9 pt white circular handles ringed in the accent and lifted by a small shadow, drawn in view points so they keep their size at any zoom. Arrows show start, end, and a bend handle at the curve's middle.
- **Crop frame:** drawn as the capture overlay draws a selection: the picture outside the frame dimmed with the room dimming (26%, 45% with Increase Contrast; only the picture, never the window), a 1.5 pt white edge over a 3.5 pt selection outline, white 0.6 thirds at 0.5 pt while dragging, and thick white corners (3 pt over a 5.5 pt outline, round caps, arms up to 18 pt).
- **Crop ratios:** each crop starts Free, the frame the whole canvas, any shape. Choosing a ratio fits the largest frame of that shape the picture holds (and the canvas, when combined images grew it), centered there, whatever the frame was, so the ratios can be tried one after another; from then on every drag keeps the shape, whatever the keys: a corner holds the corner across from it and grows along the longer of the two drags, so a new frame dragged out of nothing takes the shape from its first pixel; a side moves alone and the other side follows it about the frame's middle. Free, Shift keeps the shape the frame has when the drag starts (a square when it is dragged out of nothing), sides included. Original is the shape of the whole picture, as turned. The frame may reach past the picture, as ever, and is made whole pixels when the crop is made, so a shape can be a pixel off. Reset puts the frame back to the picture and the ratio to Free; Escape puts the frame back to the canvas and the ratio to Free.
- **Canvas lock:** Lock Canvas (the Picture menu, and the Image menu) stops the canvas from growing to hold combined images. Today only images grow it: marks of every other kind are cut off at the canvas's edge as they always were. Locked, an image combined or moved past the edge stays where it is, cut off at the edge, and the canvas keeps the size it has; a crop is the person's own act and still sets the canvas. It is kept for the next editor (the user defaults, `annotation.lockCanvas`) rather than in a capture's document or Settings: it is how the editor is used, not what the capture is. Off by default; VoiceOver hears the Picture menu item as checked.
- **Text editing:** the field matches the mark exactly (face, size, line height, color, label fill and corner) so text does not move when editing ends; no automatic substitutions; Chinese and other input methods work in place. Selected text keeps its ink: on plain and outlined text the system selection color at 0.35 (0.45 in Dark); on a label a wash opposite to the ink (white 0.35 under dark ink, black 0.22 under white ink).
- **Cursors:** crosshair for drawing tools, I-beam for Text, arrow for Select and Background, open hand over a mark (closed while moving), pointing hand over a redaction suggestion with the Redact tool (the open hand wins over a mark), I-beam over a line of text with the Highlighter once the capture's lines are in, over highlights too unless one is selected (crosshair off text and while ⌘ is held, which frees the stroke), resize cursors on handles, crosshair on arrow ends and bends.
- **Background mode:** the picture takes no clicks while the Background tool is in hand; the capsule sets everything. Escape or Return leaves the mode. The background layer is drawn at 1 px per point, only over what is on screen. Crop hides the background and shows the plain capture.
- **Combining:** images dropped (files, Quick Access cards, image data), pasted (⌘V), or added from the empty canvas's context menu (Paste, Add Image…) become image marks. While a drag hovers, a 3 pt accent ring stands just outside the picture, as Finder rings a drop target. An image has four corner handles that keep its proportions; Shift frees them. The canvas grows to hold the images unless it is locked, and canvas past the capture takes the matte.
- **Redaction suggestions:** with the Redact tool in hand, text that reading found better covered (email addresses, phone numbers, card numbers, ID numbers, keys and tokens, IP addresses, passwords, postal addresses, people's names) is outlined where its redaction would go: the text's box, fitted to the glyph ink, padded by 12% of its height (at least 2 capture pixels) on whole pixels and clipped to the crop. One outline per match, not per line, so a cover leaves the rest of the line readable. Finding aims at precision over recall: an unwanted outline costs a glance, a screenful of them hides the ones that matter. So a key needs a known prefix, a label, or a long random run; a phone number must be written as one (a "+", ten digits, or a last group of four), never a date; and a name must be written as one (two capitalized words in one script, or a common Chinese surname with a one- or two-character given name) and come from the Chinese reading, which never turns Chinese into Latin letters, so product names such as "Hermes", words such as "运维", and misread lines are left alone. Each outline is 1.5 pt accent dashes [4, 3] with 3 pt corners over the 1.5 pt suggestion outline (2 pt over 2 pt solid white under Increase Contrast), no fill, in view points, on the canvas's overlay layers above the marks and under the selection and crop frame. A click on one (a press that moves less than 4 pt) covers it with a redaction in the current method, solid black by default, as one undo step named "Redact"; Redact All in the capsule covers them all. A suggestion goes once redactions cover 90% of its visible part, or when a crop cuts it away. The outlines step aside while a redaction is dragged out by hand past 4 pt and return when it ends. The help tag over one reads its kind, then "Click to redact": Email address, Phone number, Card number, ID number, Key or token, IP address, Password, Address, Name. Suggestions are never in a copy, a save, or an export.
- **Reading for suggestions and text lines:** once per session for each, the first time the Redact tool or the Highlighter is in hand (at open when it is the remembered tool), on this Mac: the capture with any combined images, cut to the canvas and a margin of a quarter of its longer side (at least 160 px), so in place only the selection and its surroundings are read, never the whole display. Suggestions read it twice at once (Latin; Simplified Chinese with English); the highlighter once (Simplified Chinese with English, which places English too), keeping only where lines and words lie, never what they say. The two share the Chinese reading: the tool picked first starts it, and the other's needs of it are met from it, even while it is under way, so a capture both are used on is read twice, not three times, and the second tool's result comes in a fraction of the time (on the drawn demo page, the lines in about 60 ms after the suggestions instead of about 400 ms, and the suggestions in about 140 ms after the lines instead of about 340 ms). The shared reading serves while the combined images are as it saw them and it still covers what the canvas shows (a crop keeps it; a selection moved past it is read anew), and a reading neither tool waits for any longer is stopped. Nothing runs while idle or for a session that never redacts or highlights. A reading that fails is not kept: the capsule offers Try Again, and the next pickup of the tool reads again (no reading is repeated on its own, so a capture Vision cannot read never spins). A canvas leaving its window drops a reading under way, which is no failure, and the next pickup of the tool reads again; a picture combined, moved, or removed after a reading (an undo or redo included) makes it read again, at once if its tool is in hand.
- **Highlighter over text:** a highlighter drag that starts on a line of text (or within 35% of its height above or below it, or half its height past its ends) snaps to the text, as CleanShot's smart highlighter does, and runs on as a text selection does: from the word it started on to the word under the pointer, so dragging down from the end of a line carries on along the next one: the rest of the first line, every line between whole, and the last line from its start to the pointer's word (upward the same, mirrored). Whole words only (each Chinese, Japanese, or Korean character is a word, and punctuation goes with its word: "，" and "。" with the character before, "（" with the one after, so a band never starts or ends on a lone mark). The pointer belongs to the nearest line of the column the drag started in (lines above and below one another, overlapping side to side, of about one size, less than one and a half lines apart), so the turn to the next line comes halfway between the two, a pointer beyond the column's ends takes its first or last line, and the column beside it is never reached. A press on text starts a highlight even on a highlight already there, whose edges hug the lines around it: those take the press only when selected. Highlighting over a highlight of the same color extends it: bands that meet on a line become one, in the place of the uppermost of them among the marks (nothing that lay over another mark drops under it), so a word highlighted twice (a double click) is no darker and the overlap of two strokes does not show; a highlight that only covers what is highlighted already changes nothing. Another color is a highlight of its own. A click on a word highlights the word. Shift keeps the drag on its first line. Holding ⌘, at the press or midway, draws a free stroke instead, and letting go snaps it again; a drag that starts off text, or before the capture's lines are in, is drawn freely. One undo step, named "Highlight" (a free stroke's is "Draw"). VoiceOver hears when the lines are in: "N lines of text to highlight" or "No text to highlight".
- **VoiceOver:** each open suggestion is a button, "Email address, suggested redaction", that covers it when pressed; the canvas reads "Capture, N marks, N suggestions to redact" while the Redact tool is in hand and suggestions are open; the end of a reading is announced as "N suggestions to redact" or "No suggestions to redact".

### Marks
Every mark is drawn by one renderer, on screen and in exports, so what is copied is what was seen.

- **Arrows:** one filled outline per arrow, bendable along a quadratic curve. Heads are 3 × the stroke plus 4 pt long. Classic: a filled head with a notched back; tapered: a body widening from a fine tail to the head; open: a chevron over the full body; double: classic heads at both ends.
- **Lines and shapes:** round-capped lines, dashed at 1.5 × the stroke on, 2.5 × off; rectangles with 4 pt corners by default; rectangles and ellipses outlined or filled.
- **Pen and highlighter:** smooth curves through the pointer's samples; the highlighter multiplies into the picture, so text stays dark and white turns yellow. Over dark ground, where multiplying would leave nothing to see, it screens a deep tint of its color instead (the same hue at a tenth of white's luminance), the same thing turned over: the ground takes the tint and light text stays light. Dark ground is a shade darker than mid-gray covering most of what the mark covers, read from the picture with any combined images along the mark (up to 48 small squares, so a long stroke costs no more than a word); it is read again as the mark is drawn, moved, or reshaped, and when images change under it. The capsule's swatch still shows the color itself.
- **Highlight box:** a rectangle of marker color (the highlighter's yellow by default) multiplied into the picture, as the highlighter's stroke is, so text under it stays dark and white turns the color; over dark ground it screens the deep tint of the color instead, read in a grid of up to 48 small squares tiling the box (a few rows by up to 48 in all), where the highlighter reads squares along its stroke: the shade most of them share decides. Dragged from corner to corner (Shift: a square), 2 pt corners, no outline and no shadow; it has the eight handles of a rectangle and no sizes in its capsule, only colors and Delete. A press inside it starts a new mark unless it is selected, as for a redaction. Tool key M, the Highlight Box chip after the Highlighter; one undo step named "Add Highlight Box".
- **Highlights snapped to text:** one band per line, multiplied into the picture like the highlighter's strokes. A band is as tall as its line's letters reach, ascenders and descenders included, the lines of a paragraph made one height on their baselines (so a line without descenders gets as tall a band as the line under it), plus 10% of that height above and below, but never past halfway to the line above or below, so the bands of neighboring lines meet without overlapping, even in tight code; it runs past its words' ends by 20% of the height, but never past halfway to the next word, so it stops cleanly between two. Edges between neighbors fall on whole pixels. Corners are 20% of the band's height (not semicircles, which bulged over neighboring words). All of a mark's bands are one path filled once, so where two lines' bands meet the color does not double. It moves and is deleted whole and has no handles: its ends belong to words, so it is drawn again rather than reshaped. Over dark ground it screens a deep tint of its color instead, as the highlighter's strokes do, so a dark editor or terminal takes a visible band and its light text stays light.
- **Text looks:** plain; outlined (an edge of 16% of the size, at least 2 pt, in the opposite ink); label (a rounded fill in the color, corners 32% of the size, padding 40% across and 16% down, ink black 0.88 or white).
- **Counters:** a disc (16 + 3 × the stroke pt across) in the color with the label in the opposite ink, numbered 1, 2, 3; A, B, C; or I, II, III. Each numbering counts on its own (after 1, 2 the first letter is A), and a counter is placed by a click and not selected, so a change of numbering applies to the next counters.
- **Redaction:** solid (the color, black by default), blur (strong enough that text cannot be read), or pixelate (blocks at least 12 px, larger for larger areas, each nudged by noise seeded from the mark so it redraws the same and cannot be worked back). A redaction suggestion, taken with a click or Redact All, becomes an ordinary redaction over its cover in the method and color the capsule shows.
- **Spotlight:** everything outside the spots dimmed by the spotlight dimming; rectangle or oval spots.
- **Images:** combined images are part of the picture: they lie under the spotlight and every other mark, and blurs and pixelations show them under them. Shadow is off by default. When the picture is turned or flipped they turn and flip with it (the image is kept as it was added, with how it is held beside it).

### Capture Overlay (Signature)
Softly dimmed screen; selection with a white 1.5 pt edge over a dark outline; a crosshair with a 4 pt center gap and 13 pt arms, white over the same outline; a 120 pt loupe showing 15 captured pixels across (8 pt per pixel, aligned to device pixels) with a faint 0.08 grid, an accent center cell, a white rim, and the lens shadow. Window mode drops the crosshair and loupe and highlights the hovered window. Over the bar the system's arrow shows in place of the crosshair and loupe (which the bar would cover, leaving no pointer to be seen), and in window mode no window is highlighted under it; while the bar fades out under a drag, the crosshair stays.

Settings › Capture › Magnifier chooses that loupe, Magnified (the default), or Actual Size, for screenshots (All-In-One and the Self-Timer's choosing too) and Annotate Area: a lens centered on the pointer, Small (75 pt, the default), Medium (90 pt), or Large (120 pt), showing the screen at its own scale with no grid, undimmed (the picture at 0.75 over a clear backing; opaque under Reduce Transparency), its center cell the target in place of the crosshair, stroked white or black against the pixel under it over an outline of the other. The lens may run off a display's edge rather than move off its target; the color capsule stays on screen. The first press puts the lens and its capsule away on every display and the crosshair marks the pointer through the drag; a click with no drag leaves no selection and brings the lens back, so the hidden pointer is never lost. Pick Color, Capture Text, recording, and scrolling keep the magnified loupe. [Docs/capture-picker.md](Docs/capture-picker.md).

A drag can carry the selection from one display onto another: each display draws its own part of it (dimmed outside, the white edge only where the selection ends), and the size capsule appears once, on the display under the middle of the selection's bottom edge. The crosshair and loupe show only on the display the pointer is over (the key panel hears every move and the controller passes it to each display, the first press too, so an actual-size lens put away on one display stays away on the others); window mode highlights the hovered window on every display it reaches. The capture is put together from each display's part at the sharpest display's scale, clear where no display reaches. In-place annotation keeps its selection on the display it began on, where its interface lives.

While an area or a window is chosen, the overlay's panels sit at the screen-saver level, above full-screen apps, the menu bar, open menus, and the Dock: just above pop-up menus, macOS 26 and later handed presses in the menu bar's band to the menu bar, so a selection could neither start there nor be dragged into it. It still never hides macOS's Screen Recording confirmation: a frozen overlay appears only once a frame has arrived, and a live one is dismissed by a watchdog when none does. In-place annotation goes back to just above pop-up menus, under input methods' candidate windows.

The screen is frozen by default: it is captured the moment the shortcut is pressed, before the app takes the keyboard or the pointer, so hover cards, tooltips, and open menus are still in it. The overlay appears at once but lets the pointer through, without the crosshair, until the pointer's display is captured; then the frozen screen is behind it and it takes the keyboard and the pointer. What is kept is cut from that frozen screen. With Freeze off (Settings › Capture › Selecting) the overlay appears at once over the live screen and the area is captured when the drag ends. A window is always captured on its own, with its shadow and transparent corners.

Capture Area has a shortcut for each way of keeping what it takes, all in the overlay above and none with keys until chosen: Capture Area and Copy puts the capture on the clipboard and Capture Area and Save saves a copy to the export folder, each whatever Settings › General say about copying and saving and with the Quick Access card they say; Capture Area and Annotate opens it in the annotation editor, kept off the clipboard and without a card as Capture Area and Pin keeps its capture (Annotate Area marks the selection up in place instead); Capture Area and Pin and Capture Area and Upload are as before. They are the URL API's `capture-area` actions under their own names.

Snapping to text (Settings › Capture › Selecting, off by default): on a frozen screen, the edges of a selection being dragged snap to the blocks of text on it, so a drag around a paragraph lands on it with a little room all round. An edge within 8 pt of a block's edge moves to 4 pt outside it. Edges only enclose a block: one cutting into the far side of a block it encloses moves out to take that line whole, while one that nicks a block it leaves out is not moved, and none moves onto a block to leave it out. A block counts for an edge only where the selection reaches it along the other axis (a left edge ignores a block above or below the selection); a snapped edge never lies inside another block, and between two blocks closer than 8 pt it stops halfway between them. The edges are worked out from the pointer on every move, the anchor's included, so dragging away lets one go; they stay inside the display and on its pixels, and the size reading is the snapped size. A drag still under 4 pixels wide or tall (a click with a hair of movement) is never snapped, so it stays a click. A snapped edge is drawn in the accent, 2 pt over the selection's white edge (a text selection's accent edge is 1 pt, so a snapped one is 2 pt there, 3 pt under Increase Contrast), heavier as well as colored so it reads without the color. Pressing Command during a drag turns snapping off until the mouse button is released, so letting go of the key mid-drag does not throw the edges to the text, and the next drag snaps again; a corner moved with the arrow keys does the same, as it was set to the pixel by hand, and the first press goes on from the selection as the snap made it (every edge it placed, the anchor's side included, stays where it was, and only the dragged corner moves); Shift (square) and Option (from the center, except in Capture Text, where Option still opens the OCR panel) leave the shape to the pointer, and Space moves the selection as it was. It applies where the screen is frozen and an area is dragged out: Capture Area, Annotate Area (always frozen), Capture Text, Translate Area, All-In-One's first drag (unless R or S has chosen a recording or a scrolling capture first), and the Self-Timer's choosing. A live screen, window mode, a recording's or a scrolling capture's area, Pick Color, Measure, and the handles of All-In-One's framed area never snap. VoiceOver: with snapping on, the overlay's help adds "The selection's edges snap to text; hold Command to place them freely."; snapping is otherwise silent.

The blocks are found when a display's frozen frame arrives, after the overlay is shown and has the keyboard and the pointer, and nothing waits for them: not the next display's frame, not the first drag. Each display's frame goes to the system's text-rectangle detector off the main actor, at utility priority, which places a box on each line (or part of one) without reading it, in tens of milliseconds (20 to 60 ms on a drawn page of 3456 × 2234 pixels; the latency probe `overlay.textblocks`, from the frame's arrival to the blocks being in). Nothing is drawn or snapped until they are in, and ending the overlay drops what the detector finds: a detection not yet started never starts, and one running finishes first, since the system's request cannot be called back. Pure grouping turns the boxes into blocks, judging every distance in line heights so a display's scale does not matter: boxes on one row up to 2.5 line heights apart make a line (the pieces a code line is detected in), lines of one size and one column make a block when they are at most 1.1 line heights apart (a paragraph, a listing) or evenly spaced up to 2.5 (a list, a table's rows), and columns of short cells standing side by side with at least three rows lined up make one table. A line that is the only one between two paragraphs bridges neither, since the same gap on both sides is then the space between paragraphs, not the rhythm of a list. A single line narrower than 5 line heights (a button's title) is no block, so a toolbar does not make a block of every button, and a block's lines are never snapped to one by one. What the detector misses is not part of a block: a lone closing brace, so a listing can end a row above its last one. Columns of text set less than 2.5 line heights apart read as one block, and an edge between them does not snap. The blocks of every display are put together in global points, since a drag may cross displays.

What the app floats over the screen to capture (the overlay, Quick Access, readings, the color card, the countdown, a recording's outline, dimming, or frame and its control bar) is left out of every capture and recording. A recording takes in three of its windows on purpose, the keystrokes, the click rings, and the camera bubble; screenshots never do. Its standard windows (Settings, Capture History, the editor, the OCR panel) are captured like any app's, and window mode can choose them; pins follow Settings.

C copies the color under the crosshair, the magnifier's center cell, in Settings' notation and ends the capture, as in Snipaste; the Copied reading shows by the point. It works in every overlay that chooses an area once its display's frame is in (Capture Area, Capture Area and Pin, the Self-Timer's choosing, and Annotate Area until an area is chosen), and in Measure; not in window mode or Capture Text. With Option it opens the color card instead, as an Option-click does. The magnifier's reading shows the color in Settings' notation (hex by default), so it is what C copies.

Choosing an area is one drag, and the keys below only refine it; none of them is needed. They work wherever an area is dragged out (Capture Area, Capture Area and Pin, the Self-Timer's choosing, Annotate Area, Capture Text, and Record Screen's and Scrolling Capture's choosing), not in window mode, which has no crosshair.
- **Arrows:** move the crosshair and the magnifier one captured pixel, ten with Shift (a point on a standard display, half a point on a Retina one, so a selection can reach every pixel). Before the press the hidden pointer is moved along, as in Pick Color, so the press lands on the crosshair. During a drag they move the corner being dragged instead and the pointer stays where it is: the corner then stays that far from the pointer for the rest of the drag, so the next move of the mouse carries on from it, and it stops at the display's edge (a selection across displays, at their outer edges). A selection whose edges snapped to text is nudged from the selection as shown, not from the pointer: the edges stay and only the dragged corner moves, by a pixel or ten. A pointer beyond the edge, on the next display, leaves the corner at the edge, and the arrows move it from there at once. The size reading, the crosshair, and the magnifier follow the corner (an actual-size lens is put away by then), and C takes the color under it. Option keeps its say (a selection from the center grows both ways). Shift is also the shape lock, so a Shift step during a drag comes with the shape held: along the side that follows the longer one it moves nothing, and a key that moves nothing, or that the display's edge stops, is not kept, so letting go of Shift brings back the free selection with no distance hidden behind it. A free step of ten is a held arrow, which repeats. While Space moves the selection no corner is being set, and the arrows do nothing. VoiceOver hears the new size after each key.
- **Shift:** keeps the selection to a square, or to the shape Settings › Capture › Selecting › Shift keeps names (A square by default; 4:3, 3:2, 16:10, 16:9, 21:9, 3:4, 2:3, 9:16). The longer side leads. At a display's edge the shape holds by shrinking to fit; it is never cut to another one. A side not dragged at all (a drag straight along an edge) grows toward the room there is: away from the edge it began on.
- **Tab:** opens the size control, Capture Area's and Annotate Area's own (All-In-One frames its area after its drag, and window mode, Capture Text, recording, and scrolling pass Tab on). It is All-In-One's frame with a smaller bar: the drag in hand becomes the area, on the display it began on, as it is; with no drag, an area a third of the display across is framed around the crosshair. A drag made with Shift held was kept to a shape, so it opens in that shape (the one Settings keep, a square by default) with the chip naming it; any other opens free, with the chip on its symbol alone. The frame has the corner arms, the movable area, the handles, the thirds while adjusted, and the arrows, with Option to resize, as All-In-One's (see All-In-One). Its bar holds Capture (`camera.viewfinder`, titled, semibold), the width and height fields in pixels, the ratio chip (`aspectratio`, its menu and its shape kept as All-In-One's), and Cancel (`xmark`). The keys stay with the frame, so Return captures the area as the drag would have (frozen as it was frozen; Annotate Area opens the annotation where the area is) and Escape ends an adjustment, then cancels; Tab goes to the width, to type it, and Return there sets the size and gives the keys back to the frame, so the next Return captures. The letter keys and ⌘C and ⌘S of All-In-One are not its own. A shape chosen on the chip (not Any Shape) is also the one Shift keeps from then on, as Settings › Capture › Selecting › Shift keeps shows; a square is that setting's A square. VoiceOver reads the bar as "Area size", the frame as "Capture area" with its size, and its help as "Press Return to capture the area. The arrow keys move the area; with Option they resize it. Tab goes to its width and height in pixels, to type them, and the aspect ratio button keeps a shape. Press Escape to cancel."

The pointer is left out of screenshots, as macOS's own are, unless Settings › Capture › Selecting › Show the pointer in screenshots is on. Then an area, the whole screen, and a window are captured with it where it is, and a frozen screen of Capture Area and Annotate Area is captured with it where it was when the shortcut was pressed: the overlay hides the real pointer and draws its crosshair, so the frozen picture behind it shows the pointer at that old place, and what is kept is cut from that picture. The magnifier and C read that same picture, so over the pointer's old place they read the pointer's pixels. Text, Pick Color, and Measure read the screen's pixels and never take it. All-In-One's frozen screen and the Self-Timer's carry none: All-In-One may read its area's text from the frozen picture, so an All-In-One screenshot taken from a frozen screen leaves the pointer out (taken from a live one it shows it), and the Self-Timer captures its area again once the time is up, live, with the pointer where it is then.

### All-In-One Bar (Signature)
A Liquid Glass capsule with an opaque window-colored backing inside, a 0.5 pt separator hairline, and the floating bar shadow. With Reduce Transparency it is a solid window-colored capsule with the same hairline.

### All-In-One
One shortcut for every way to take an area. All-In-One opens the capture overlay as a screenshot does (frozen when Freeze says so, crosshair and loupe), with All-In-One's modes added to its bar: Area, Window, Screen | Text, Scrolling, Record | Cancel, chips as the bar's others (Text `text.viewfinder`, Scrolling `arrow.up.and.down.text.horizontal`, Record `record.circle`). Letters choose them before an area: A, W or Space, F, O, S, R. How the area is chosen (Area or Window) and what it is for (Text, Scrolling, Record) are marked apart, each with the selected chip, so Window and Text can both be on; choosing the purpose in hand again, or Area (A), lets it go back to a plain capture, and each change is announced. An area is chosen on one display.

- **Choosing:** once the drag ends the area stays, framed as Scrolling Capture's choosing is: the screen dimmed 26% around it, the in-place frame's corner arms, movable, resizable by its handles or dragged anew outside it, its size in pixels as a reading above its corner. The arrows move it a point (ten with Shift); with Option they move its right or bottom edge.
- **The bar under it:** a `CapsuleBar` placed as the scrolling bar is (under the area, centered, above it or inside its foot when there is no room): Capture (`camera.viewfinder`, titled, semibold) | Copy, Save, Annotate, Pin, and Upload (only while a destination is set up), icon-only 30 pt chips | Capture Text, Scrolling Capture, Record | Cancel. Tooltips name each one and its key: Return (or a double click inside) captures, Command-C and Command-S copy and save, E annotates, P pins, U uploads, O copies the text, S starts a scrolling capture, R records, F takes the whole display. Escape ends an adjustment, else cancels.
- **Size and shape:** after Record, the bar carries the area's width and height in pixels as two fields in the reading's face (12 pt medium, fixed-width figures), 50 pt each around a secondary-ink "×": their digits hug the "×" (the width right-aligned, the height left-aligned) and read like a reading until touched. Behind each a capsule as tall as the chips (24 pt, fully rounded) takes the chips' hover fill under the pointer, with an I-beam, and their selected fill while typed in, doubled under Increase Contrast. Only digits go in, full-width ones too, at most five, an input method's composing left alone until it is done; Return or leaving a field sets the area from its top-left corner, kept on the display, and both fields then show the size the area took; Escape puts the size back; Tab from the height, Shift-Tab from the width, Return, or Escape give the keys back to the frame. A chip or a shape chosen while a size is typed takes the typed size first. While the area is adjusted the bar steps aside and the size reading above the area's corner takes its place; otherwise the bar's fields are the only size shown. Then the ratio chip (`aspectratio`, icon-only): its menu offers Any Shape, 1:1, then 4:3, 3:2, 16:10, 16:9, 21:9, then 3:4, 2:3, 9:16, the shape in hand checked. A shape kept reshapes the area around its middle at once, holds through the handles (a corner grows from the opposite one, an edge resizes its side with the other following, centered), a new drag, Option-arrows, and a typed width or height (the other side follows), and stops at the display's edge rather than change shape; the chip then names it ("16:9") beside its accent symbol, on the selected fill. The menu shows above the overlay: the panel steps below pop-up menus while it is open, as the recording bar's menus do. Capture Area's own size control (Tab, see Capture Overlay) has this same pair of fields and this same menu.
- **What follows:** a capture is kept as any screenshot is, Copy and Save on top of what Settings say, Annotate and Pin kept off the clipboard and without a card, as the URL API's actions are; a frozen screen gives the area as it was frozen. Text is read as Capture Text reads a selection; a scrolling capture starts at once on the area; Record turns the same overlay into Screen Recording's choosing, live, with its bar under the area.
- **Remembered:** the area last taken comes back framed the next time, on the same display arranged the same way (Settings › Capture › Selecting, on by default). The whole display is not kept.
- **VoiceOver:** the size fields read as "Width in pixels" and "Height in pixels", the ratio chip as "Aspect Ratio" with the shape kept ("16:9", or "Any shape") as its value. The overlay announces "All-In-One. Drag over an area, then choose what to do with it."; a mode chosen before an area is announced ("Text. Drag over text to copy it."); the framed area reads as "Capture area" with its size, its help naming the keys; the bar reads as "All-In-One", each icon-only chip by its name.

### Window Screenshots
A window is captured on its own, its corners clear, with its shadow unless Settings › Capture › Windows turns that off. What it is put on is chosen there: the background every new screenshot gets (the default), none, or the desktop wallpaper. On the wallpaper, the window sits on the part of the desktop picture around it as the screen shows it (taken from the wallpaper's own windows, so dynamic and photo wallpapers alike, without icons or windows), as large as the finished picture with the background's room (0.08 of the capture's measure) all round and cut to the display, and lined up with the window where it was (its shadow falls deeper below it than above, so the piece is centered on the capture with its shadow, not on the window); the piece is kept as a JPEG of at most 4096 pixels across with the backgrounds' pictures, once per picture, so the capture is marked up and kept the same later, and goes again once nothing uses it (no capture's marks in the history, no saved background, not the last picture chosen for one; never while an editor is open). A window keeps its own corners and shadow on it, as on any background; with the shadow turned off, none is drawn under it either. Shift held as the window is clicked (also for the Self-Timer) takes it the other way round: no background where there would be one, the wallpaper where there would be none. When the wallpaper cannot be taken the window is kept with none.

### In-Place Annotation (Annotate Area)
Marking up a selection where it lies, without opening a window. Annotate Area (⇧⌘6) opens the capture overlay as for a screenshot: frozen screen, All-In-One bar, crosshair and loupe. Once an area or a window is chosen, the overlay's own selection layers, readings, crosshair, loupe, and bar step aside, the other displays' panels go away, and the editor's canvas takes over at the screen's own size over the frozen screen: no zoom, no window chrome, no combining, no turning, flipping, or resizing, and no crop or background.

- **Selection chrome:** vector layers, drawn as the canvas's crop frame is: the screen around the selection dimmed with the room dimming (26%, 45% under Increase Contrast), a 1.5 pt white edge over a 3.5 pt selection outline, thick white corners (3 pt over a 5.5 pt outline, round caps, arms up to 18 pt, never more than a third of a side), and white 0.6 thirds at 0.5 pt while the selection is adjusted. Where the selection meets the display's edge, the frame steps in by 4.5 pt, so its edge and corners stay on screen to be seen and grabbed. The size reading is a reading capsule ("1616 × 1216", pixels).
- **Toolbar:** the rail laid flat, in the All-In-One manner: a 42 pt Liquid Glass capsule with an opaque window-colored backing, a 0.5 pt separator hairline (the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast), and the floating bar shadow; solid under Reduce Transparency, swapped live. 30 pt circular chips 6 pt inside and 2 pt apart, each a 14 pt medium symbol in label ink, no labels; tooltips name the tool and its key (the Highlighter's also how it meets text). Groups split by 18 pt separators with 6 pt around them: Arrow, Line, Rectangle, Ellipse, Pen, Highlighter, Highlight Box | Text, Counter | Redact, Spotlight | Undo, Redo (tertiary ink when disabled) | Open in Editor (`arrow.up.forward.square`), Save (`square.and.arrow.down`), Cancel (`xmark`), Copy (`checkmark` in the accent). There is no Select chip: with no tool in hand the selection itself is adjusted. The tool in hand takes the selected chip fill and an accent symbol; clicking it again puts it down. Hover takes the hover fill; Increase Contrast the contrast fills.
- **Style capsule:** the editor's, unchanged, under the toolbar for the tool in hand or the selected mark. With the Redact tool the canvas outlines its suggestions and the capsule carries Finding text… and Redact All, as in the editor.
- **Adjusting:** with no tool in hand, a drag inside moves the selection and its corners and edges resize it (corners reach 12 pt and edges 6 pt, shrinking to a quarter and a sixth of the frame on small selections, never under 4 and 3 pt); a drag outside chooses a new area while nothing is drawn. With a tool in hand, only presses on the frame line or outside it resize, so marks run to the edge. The toolbar hides while the selection is adjusted.
- **Keys:** the editor's tool keys, Delete, arrows, and Undo work as in the editor. Return, Enter, or a double-click copies. Escape cancels an adjustment, then puts the tool down, then cancels.
- **Outcomes:** Copy puts the marked selection on the clipboard, into Capture History (the plain selection kept beside the marks, still editable), and in a Quick Access card. Save does the same and also saves to the export folder. Open in Editor keeps it in the history and opens the editor with the marks editable, without the clipboard or a card. The picture reaches the clipboard before anything is written; a failure beeps and is announced.
- **VoiceOver:** the area announces "Mark up the selection. Press Return to copy it, or Escape to cancel." when it starts; its value is "W × H pixels", posted again after each adjustment; the toolbar reads as "Mark Up".

### Self-Timer Countdown
Self-Timer opens the capture overlay as for a screenshot (frozen by default, All-In-One bar, crosshair and loupe); once an area or a window is chosen the overlay leaves, and the countdown shows where the capture will be while the screen is set up: a menu opened, a card hovered. It takes neither the keyboard nor the pointer, which would close the very menu being set up.

- **Outline:** the chosen area (a window's frame for a window) drawn as the capture overlay draws a selection: a white 1.5 pt edge over the 3.5 pt dark outline (0.32; 0.7 under Increase Contrast). Nothing is dimmed: the screen is in use. One outline panel per display the area reaches.
- **Disc:** 64 pt, Liquid Glass over an opaque window-colored backing, as the All-In-One bar is; a solid window-colored disc under Reduce Transparency. The seconds left in the reading face at 26 pt, label ink, centered. A 2.5 pt ring in the accent, inset 6 pt, round caps, starting at twelve o'clock, runs out over the whole countdown over a faint track of label ink at 0.12 (0.3 under Increase Contrast). Rim: the 0.5 pt separator hairline, or the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast. The floating bar shadow, with 24 pt of room around the disc in its panel. The first second is on the disc before it shows, so it never appears blank; seconds are counted to absolute deadlines and never drift.
- **Placement:** centered 12 pt below the area; above it when there is no room below; inside its bottom edge only when neither fits, since inside covers what is being set up. Always on a display, 8 pt from its edges.
- **Panels:** borderless and clear, just above pop-up menus so an opened menu cannot cover them, on every Space, never key, never activating the app, and letting every click through. Like every panel the app floats over the screen, they are left out of the capture.
- **Ending:** at zero the countdown leaves the screen and the capture is taken live, the screen as it is then, whatever was frozen while choosing; it is named after the app in front at that moment. An area is cut from the screen; a window is captured on its own. If another capture is being chosen at zero, nothing is captured.
- **Cancelling:** the Self-Timer shortcut again, or the status menu entry, which reads "Cancel Self-Timer" while the countdown runs. Starting another capture cancels it too. Nothing is captured.
- **Delay:** 2, 3, 5, 10, or 15 seconds, 5 by default (Settings › Capture › Self-Timer). Self-Timer has no shortcut by default.
- **VoiceOver:** the disc is not an element. Starting announces "Capturing in N seconds. Press [the shortcut's keys] again to cancel.", or "Capturing in N seconds. Choose Cancel Self-Timer in the menu bar to cancel." when it has no shortcut; cancelling announces "Self-Timer cancelled."

### Pick Color
One pixel's color off the screen, in the capture overlay's world. The overlay is always frozen, whatever Freeze says, and shows the screen as it is: no dimming, no selection outline, no All-In-One bar, since it chooses no area. The crosshair and the magnifier follow the pointer as in a capture. While picking, the magnifier's center cell uses the capture loupe's adaptive 1.5 pt stroke: white over dark sampled pixels, black over light pixels, with a 3 pt opposite-color outline (0.32; 0.7 under Increase Contrast), so the cell a click copies holds over any color, the accent's included. The magnifier's reading shows the pixel and the color in the notation a click copies ("812, 344   rgb(51, 102, 255)").

- **Taking:** a click, Return, Enter, or C copies the color in Settings' notation and closes; the Copied reading says so by the point, with the swatch. With Option, a click, Return, or C opens the color card instead, which copies nothing by itself.
- **Notations:** HEX ("#3366FF", the default), RGB ("rgb(51, 102, 255)"), and HSL ("hsl(225, 100%, 60%)") are sRGB, clamped; OKLCH ("oklch(57.3% 0.234 265.3)") and Display P3 ("color(display-p3 0.25 0.395 0.964)") keep a wide-gamut color's true value. Colors are read in the frame's own space; within 3/255 of sRGB's edges counts as inside.
- **Keys:** the arrows move the crosshair one captured pixel, ten with Shift. The hidden pointer is moved along, so the mouse carries on from the crosshair; a nudge past a display's edge crosses onto the neighboring display when there is one, else stops at the edge. Escape cancels.
- **VoiceOver:** help reads "Click or press Return to copy the color under the crosshair; with Option, see it in every notation. The arrow keys move the crosshair a pixel, ten with Shift. Press Escape to cancel."; the overlay's value is the color in the chosen notation, announced after each nudge.

### Copied Reading
What was just copied, as a reading capsule by the point it came from: "Copied #3366FF" with the color's swatch after Pick Color or C, "Copied 120 × 36 pt" without one after Measure's Return. Its panel sits one level above the overlay's screen-saver level, so it reads over a measuring session still up, on every Space; it takes no clicks and no focus. It shows at once, stays 0.9 s, and fades over 0.12 s, as a pin's reading does. VoiceOver hears its text. Measure's ⌘C does not use it: the ruler's own reading says "Copied".

### Color Card
The color in every notation, its contrast against a second color, and Pin, opened by an Option-click (or Option-Return) in Pick Color. A 368 pt card, wide enough for Display P3's notation to show whole, in the text result card's materials: the capsule surface (white 0.97; Dark a 0.17 gray at 0.96), the 0.5 pt reading hairline (the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast), 12 pt continuous corners, the floating card shadow with 34 pt of room for it, and 14 pt padding.

- **Header:** the 28 pt swatch in the color's own space; the title, the color in the chosen notation (Display P3 when that notation is HEX, RGB, or HSL and the color lies outside sRGB), middle-truncated; a 24 pt close chip at the right. Outside sRGB only, a subtitle in the field label face: "Outside sRGB: HEX, RGB, and HSL are the closest"; without it the title centers on the swatch.
- **Rows:** after a one-pixel separator hairline, five 26 pt rows, HEX, RGB, HSL, OKLCH, Display P3: the name in a 74 pt column, the value in label ink, and a 24 pt copy chip (`doc.on.doc`, tooltip "Copy HEX") whose symbol turns to a checkmark for 1.2 s. VoiceOver hears "Copied …".
- **Contrast:** Contrast… (`circle.lefthalf.filled`) picks a second color in Pick Color's overlay while the card holds; it then reads "Pick Another…". A second hairline and three rows follow: Background (Text once swapped) with a 16 pt swatch, the hex, and a Swap chip (`arrow.left.arrow.right`, "Make #3366FF the Background"); WCAG 2, the ratio rounded down to a tenth ("3.9:1", so a failing pair never reads as passing) with its level (AAA; AA, AAA for large text; AA for large text only; Fails AA); APCA, "Lc 64" (a true minus for light text on dark) with what it allows (Any text, Body text, Large or bold text, Headlines, Placeholder and disabled text, Too low for text). The card's color is the text until swapped.
- **Buttons:** Contrast… and Pin (`pin`), 26 pt chips 8 pt apart, 12 pt under the rows. Pin makes a color pin and closes the card.
- **Panel:** borderless, non-activating, a level above pins (as Quick Access is), on every Space and over full-screen apps; clicking it never brings the app forward. It takes the keys only when opened from the keyboard or with VoiceOver on: then the lead notation's copy chip has focus, Escape closes, ⌘C copies the lead notation (its chip shows the checkmark), and Tab moves through the chips with Full Keyboard Access.
- **Staying:** gone after 15 s left alone, 60 s with VoiceOver; held while hovered or while the second color is picked. VoiceOver reads it as a group, "Color " and the title, announced as it opens; the contrast is announced as it comes in and on Swap.

### Measure
A PixelSnap-style ruler on the frozen screen. The overlay is always frozen, undimmed, without the All-In-One bar or the magnifier.

- **Lines:** from the pointer's pixel out to a supported RGB boundary each way: strong changes retain precise endpoints; faint and antialiased edges use nearby scanlines and gradient checks. Through the middle of the pixel on whole device pixels: 1 pt accent lines over the 3 pt ruler outline, with 8 pt caps across each end (2 pt over 4 pt solid white under Increase Contrast). They cross at the pointer in place of the crosshair, which shows only before the display's pixels are in or while a box is up.
- **Readings:** reading capsules, the length by each line ("120 pt"): points to a tenth without a trailing ".0", or whole pixels ("61 px").
- **Box:** a drag draws a box on whole pixels, accent edge over the ruler outline with the accent wash inside, at 0.6 opacity while dragged. Let go, supported contours find the content's bounds, with an alignment tap on the trackpad, or it stays as drawn without a credible boundary; either way it is then solid, with its size below it. Tools settings can disable snapping; hold Command, Shift, Option, or Control on release to keep a single manually drawn box. A box keeps the screen to itself, no lines, until a click clears it. Selections above 250,000 physical pixels find their edges off the main thread, with a Finding edges reading; a new drag, a tolerance change, or closing the session cancels stale work.
- **Tolerance:** Zero, Low, Medium (default), and High permit neighboring channel changes of 0, 7, 13, and 26 levels out of 255. T cycles them; +/− adjusts with limits. The choice is remembered across sessions and synchronized across displays. A reading capsule centered 18 pt above the display's bottom shows "Tolerance: Medium · T / + / −". Changing tolerance recomputes a snapped box from its original drag; a manual box stays manual. Settings → Tools provides the snapping toggle and tolerance picker. See [measurement snapping](Docs/measurement-snapping.md) for behavior, validation, and limits.
- **Keys:** the arrows as in Pick Color. ⌘C copies the measurement (the box's size, else the two lines' "W × H") and the overlay stays, its reading saying "Copied" for 0.9 s; Return copies and closes, with the Copied reading by the crosshair; Tab or U switches between points (the default) and pixels, remembered as Settings' Measure in; C takes the color under the crosshair as Pick Color does; Escape cancels.
- **Pixels:** a display's pixels are copied out off the main thread the first time the pointer comes to it, and let go when the pointer leaves for another display.
- **VoiceOver:** help reads "Point at something to measure it, or drag a box around it. Command-C copies the measurement, Return copies it and closes, and Tab switches between points and pixels. T cycles edge tolerance; plus and minus adjust it. The arrow keys move a pixel. Press Escape to cancel."; the value is the measurement and the tolerance ("48 × 48 pt. Tolerance: Medium."), or the measurement and "Finding edges…" while a large box is read, announced after a box, a unit or tolerance switch, or a nudge.

### Screen Recording
Recording is a capture that runs on: Record Screen opens the capture overlay (live, never frozen), an area is dragged, a click takes the whole display, Space a window, and the same overlay then frames the choice and holds the recording bar until Record. Nothing the app shows is in the video except the keystrokes and the camera, which are there on purpose.

- **Choosing:** the screen dimmed 26% with the choice clear, framed with the in-place frame's corner arms (an area only: it can still be moved, resized by its handles, or dragged anew outside it) and its size in pixels as a reading above its corner ("1600 × 1000", "App  ·  W × H" for a window). Return or a double-click inside records; Escape ends an adjustment, else cancels the choosing. The panel sits just above pop-up menus, as in-place annotation does.
- **Recording bar:** the All-In-One bar's capsule (glass over an opaque window-colored backing, solid under Reduce Transparency, 0.5 pt separator hairline, 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast, the floating bar shadow), 42 pt tall with 30 pt chips 2 pt apart, centered 12 pt under the frame, above it without room, 40 pt inside its bottom edge when neither has room. In order: Video, GIF, and Studio as mode chips (at least 84 pt, symbol 13 pt medium, `video`, `photo.stack`, and `wand.and.sparkles`, the Control Label face); a separator; Computer Audio (`speaker.wave.2`), Microphone (`mic`), Camera (`web.camera`), Show Clicks (`cursorarrow.click.2`), and Show Keystrokes (`keyboard`), 30 pt icon-only chips, symbols 14 pt medium; a separator; Record, at least 92 pt, `record.circle.fill` in white on the system red, the bar's one red, with its label in 12 pt semibold; and Cancel (`xmark`, 13 pt). A chip that is on takes the selected fill and an accent symbol.
- **Stepping back:** chips that cannot apply show tertiary ink and say why in their tooltip: the sound chips while GIF is chosen ("GIFs have no sound", their state kept for the next movie), the keystrokes and camera for a window recorded on its own (Studio draws the keys itself, so a window recorded for it keeps them; its camera chip says "Studio doesn’t add the camera to a window recorded on its own yet"). Without Input Monitoring or camera access, those chips step back the same way but can still be pressed: the first press asks macOS, later ones say in the tooltip where access is turned on. Access is never asked once recording has begun, where its prompt would be recorded.
- **Microphone and camera menus:** a press opens a menu rather than toggling: No Microphone, then the Mac's microphones (the system's default checked until one is chosen by name); No Camera, the Mac's cameras (built in, attached, and iPhones nearby), a Shape section (Circle, Rounded Square), a Size section (Small, Medium, Large), and Mirror. While a menu is open the overlay's panel steps just below pop-up menus. A microphone gone by the time recording starts gives way to the default.
- **Countdown:** the Self-Timer disc, centered under the area (Settings › Recording › Countdown: None, 3, or 5 seconds, 3 by default), with the area marked as While recording says.
- **While recording:** an area is marked from the countdown to the end as Settings › Recording › Recorded area chooses, in click-through panels on every Space, left out of the recording as all the app's windows are (but for the keystrokes, the click rings, and the camera): Outline, the default, Self-Timer's outline (a white 1.5 pt edge over the 3.5 pt dark outline), nothing dimmed; Dim the rest of the screen, the Room Dimming over all of each display the area reaches, the area clear and no edge drawn, in a panel just under the status bar's level, so the control bar, the keystrokes, and the click rings are not dimmed, with the countdown disc and the camera bubble above it (a camera that fills the area lies a level under the dim, in the area it leaves clear); or Red frame, the Recording Frame, 3 pt, wholly outside the area, with 4 pt of room around it in its panel. A window or a whole display has none. The control bar is a glass capsule 42 pt tall in the bar's materials: an 8 pt dot (system red, tertiary ink while paused), the time in the reading face at 13 pt semibold ("0:42", secondary ink while paused), while the microphone is recorded its meter (the system's `mic.and.signal.meter.fill` at 13 pt in label ink, its dots lit by how loud it has been, in fifths: up at once, falling back over about 0.3 s, dark while paused; when the microphone has sent no sound for 5 s of recording, never in the first 5 s after starting or resuming, it gives way to the system's `mic.slash` at 13 pt in system orange, tooltip "The microphone stopped sending sound. It may have been disconnected.", until sound comes again or the recording is paused), then Pause or Resume, Restart, Delete, and Stop (red) as 30 pt icon chips. It goes 12 pt under the area, above it without room, and inside a whole display's bottom edge, 32 pt up, or above the keystrokes when they show there; it is at the status bar's level (over the Dock), never key, can be dragged by its background, and fades in over 0.12 s. Settings › Recording › Show controls can turn it off.
- **Menu bar:** while recording, the icon gives way to `stop.circle` and the time in monospaced digits ("0:42", "Paused 0:42"), and a click stops; shown even when Settings hides the icon. While a GIF is made, `photo.stack` and how far along it is ("45%"), with the menu still there; the icon comes back when the card does. While a Studio movie is exported after its window closed, `wand.and.sparkles` and how far along it is, and the menu offers Cancel Studio Export under Record Screen (shown only then).
- **Keystrokes:** a large reading capsule, its materials and face (white 0.96 or a 0.17 gray at 0.94, the reading hairline, the floating reading shadow), 52 pt tall, the keys in the reading face at 26 pt semibold with 22 pt side padding, centered 28 pt above the bottom of what is recorded, at the status bar's level. A shortcut shows its modifiers in the Mac's order and its key ("⇧⌘4", "⌃⌥←"); typing runs on as typed ("Hello↩"), keeping its last 24 characters after an ellipsis; special keys by their symbols (↩ ⇥ ⌫ ⎋ arrows) and F1 to F20 by name; a key held down shows once. All keys, or Shortcuts only (keys held with ⌘, ⌃, or ⌥, and the function keys). It fades in and out over 0.12 s, 1.4 s after the last key. Keys typed into password fields never reach it.
- **Camera bubble:** the camera's picture clipped to a circle or a rounded square (corners 20% of its side), 120, 180, or 260 pt (never more than half of what is recorded), ringed by a 3 pt white line at 0.95, with a soft shadow (black 0.28, blur 12, 4 pt down) and 28 pt of room for it in its panel; mirrored as a mirror shows by default. Before the first frame it is the capsule surface (white 0.97 or a 0.17 gray), never a dark disc. Under Increase Contrast a 1 pt dark hairline (black 0.35, white 0.5 in Dark) runs outside the ring. It shows as soon as the camera is chosen, 24 pt in from the bottom-left corner of what is recorded, so it can be placed before recording, and can be dragged anywhere on its display. Double-click fills what is recorded with the camera (square, no ring or shadow) and again brings the bubble back, through a 0.12 s fade out and in. Its panel is two levels above pop-up menus, over the overlay, the Dock, and menus; filling a recording, it goes under the menu bar so the menu bar and the control bar stay within reach. The camera runs only while the bubble shows.
- **Click rings:** with Show Clicks on and Settings › Recording › Clicks set to Accent rings, each press inside what is recorded draws a ring there instead of the system's circle: 54 pt, a 3 pt accent line (0.9; 0.55 for other buttons than the main one) over an accent fill at 0.18, widening from a third of its size as it fades over 0.4 s, ease-out; under Reduce Motion it shows at its size for 0.4 s and goes. Its clear, click-through window lies over what is recorded at the status bar's level and is recorded with it; presses on the app's own windows (the control bar) draw nothing. A window recorded on its own keeps the system's circles.
- **Studio:** chosen on the bar, before recording, since a cursor recorded into the picture can't be drawn again. Recorded without the cursor and without the system's click circles, which Studio draws again, and beside the picture what the pointer did: its place 60 times a second, its look, every click, and the keys as Settings › Recording › Keystrokes chooses (under Shortcuts only, a key typed alone is kept only as a press, never as the key), so Studio can show them afterwards. They are written down whether or not Show Keystrokes was on, while Input Monitoring is on and Settings › Recording › Studio › Keep the keys pressed is (off by default, as what is typed is kept only when asked for), except for a window recorded on its own, whose keys are written down only when Show Keystrokes was on, since what is typed in other apps is no part of it; under All keys what was typed is kept as typed, in the project's telemetry beside the recording on this Mac; Studio shows them as the bar's Show Keystrokes was. Never written down: keys typed while the system keeps keystrokes private (a password field has focus, an app has turned on secure input), which the system keeps from the recording and which are dropped all the same; and Input Monitoring is never asked for once recording has begun. Stopping opens the Studio window at once, with no card or clipboard until its first movie is exported.
- **GIF:** chosen on the bar; recorded as a movie and made into a GIF when stopped, as large as things were on screen (half a Retina recording's pixels), at most 1920 pixels on its longer side, at Settings' frame rate. Copied and given a card as a movie is.
- **After recording:** what happens to a kept recording is Settings › Recording › After recording, apart from the screenshots' After screenshot in Screenshots: Copy (the file on the clipboard), Quick Access (its card), Save (a copy in the export folder), and Upload after them only while a destination exists. The first launch that knows them starts each as the screenshots' choice then was, so no recording is kept differently than before; a new install has Copy and Quick Access on and Save and Upload off. A recording that is made, a Studio movie exported for the first time, and a clip saved from the replay all follow them; so does the card a failed upload brings back. With all of them off a warning line reads "Recordings go only to History.", or "Recordings won’t be kept anywhere." with history set to Never. A copied recording is a reference to its file in the history, so with history set to Never the file stays while the clipboard holds it, as it stays while a card, the editor, or an upload does, and goes within a few seconds of the clipboard holding anything else (or at the next launch).
- **VoiceOver:** the bar reads as "Recording"; Video and GIF are radio buttons, the other chips check boxes (the microphone and camera pop-up buttons) whose value is On or Off; the frame's help reads "Press Return to start recording, or Escape to cancel."; starting announces how to stop ("Recording. Press [the shortcut's keys] again to stop."), and stopping, pausing, deleting, making a GIF, and the microphone going silent ("The microphone stopped sending sound. It may have been disconnected.", once, also when Show controls is off) are announced; while the meter shows that symbol it reads as the same sentence.

### Recording Window
A standard window for a kept recording: plays it, and makes the few edits a recording needs before it goes out. Opened by double-clicking a recording's card or its card in Capture History, or Edit… in a card's or History's menu (a Studio recording opens in Studio instead).

- **Window:** titled, unified toolbar, sized to the recording as large as it was on screen plus the 52 pt toolbar (within 85% of the screen, at least 300 pt tall of movie, and at least 760 pt wide, the window's minimum, so the whole toolbar fits as the editor's does; a GIF's window, with only Save and Copy, 560). The movie fills it in an AVPlayerView with the system's inline controls, which float over the movie. A GIF only plays here, in an image view, with Save and Copy.
- **Toolbar:** at the leading edge, Trim (`timeline.selection`, QuickTime's trimming bar with its own Trim and Cancel), Sound (`speaker.wave.2`, or `speaker.slash` while left out; only for a recording with sound), Volume beside it (a slider 80 pt wide without tick marks, silence to full in whole percents, dimmed while the sound is left out, only for a recording with sound; VoiceOver reads "Volume" and its percent), Size, a menu labelled with the size it makes in monospaced digits ("1000 × 640"): Full Size, Three Quarters, Half Size, Quarter Size, each with its size, and Quality beside it, a menu labelled with the quality it makes ("Medium Quality"): High (larger file), Medium (as recorded), Low (smaller file). Quality and Volume are not part of a GIF made of the recording. At the trailing edge, Make GIF (`photo.stack`), Save as a split button (Save a Copy, ⌘S; its menu: Save As…, Open in QuickTime Player or Open in Preview, Revert to Original), a fixed space, and Copy, `.glassProminent` with its title and symbol. Copy and Save say Copied and Saved with a checkmark for 1.5 s.
- **Keeping edits:** edits are kept in the recording when it is copied or saved and when the window closes, as the editor keeps marks; the recording as it was made stays beside it in the history, and Revert to Original brings it back (or forgets edits not kept yet). A trim, a smaller size, or a quality other than Medium is encoded again in the recording's codec, at the encoder's constant quality 0.6 for Medium (the recorder's own), 0.8 for High, and 0.45 for Low (Studio's best quality and smaller file); leaving out the sound or making it quieter alone copies the video, a quieter sound being decoded and encoded again. The volume plays as it will be kept, through the player's audio mix, so the player's own volume stays the listener's; a volume brought back to full, like Medium, is no edit. Make GIF makes a GIF of the part and size chosen, never larger than on screen, as a capture of its own with a card. A failure beeps and says so in an alert, an alert of its own when the window has closed.
- **Busy:** while edits are kept or a GIF is made, a small card over the movie (the window background, 12 pt corners, the 0.5 pt separator hairline, a soft shadow) holds a spinner and what is happening in callout secondary ("Saving the recording…", "Making the GIF…", "Reverting…"); the toolbar is disabled meanwhile.

### Studio
The window that films a Studio recording again: zooms that follow the pointer, the cursor drawn smooth and larger or in another style, clicks as a ring or ripples in a color of their own, keys, the camera in a bubble, beside the recording, or filling the movie, on a background. Opened when a Studio recording stops, and by Edit… on its card or in History. The preview plays the same composition the export writes, so what plays is what is kept.

- **Recording for Studio:** the camera chip works as for Video: its bubble shows on screen to be placed and watched, but stays out of the picture, and the camera is recorded beside the screen in a movie of its own, its pauses cut out alike, so Studio can place it without zooming it. Double-clicking the bubble to fill what is recorded is written down with it; Studio shows those times as camera-only layouts on its timeline.

- **Window:** titled, unified toolbar, 1180 × 780 pt (at least 900 × 600). The preview fills the top left on the under-page ground, the movie fitted 20 pt inside it and edged with the picture border (the 0.5 pt separator; the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast); the transport under it; the timeline, 128 pt (156 pt while the recording has a camera that is not side by side, for the layouts' lane), along the bottom; on the right the inspector, 290 pt, a grouped form.
- **Toolbar:** as the Recording Window's: Save as a split button (Save a Copy, ⌘S; its menu: Save As…), a fixed space, and Copy, `.glassProminent` with its title and symbol; Copied and Saved with a checkmark for 1.5 s.

A spinner over it until the first movie is made. - **Transport:** Play or Pause (Space), then the time in callout secondary with monospaced digits ("0:01.2 / 0:03.5": the movie's time, cuts left out; VoiceOver reads "1.2 of 3.5 seconds"); at the trailing edge Add Zoom (`plus.magnifyingglass`: two seconds at the playhead, in the room the other zooms leave: after any that hold the playhead's place, those touching them included), Add Layout (`rectangle.on.rectangle`, only while the camera's layout lane shows: three seconds at the playhead, in the room the other layouts leave (after any that hold the playhead's place, those touching them included), camera only, or the bubble where the layout is camera only already) and Cut (`scissors`, enabled while a stretch is chosen). - **Timeline:** on the control background, 16 pt in from each side. A ruler: 6 pt tertiary ticks, labels in 10 pt secondary with monospaced digits at a step that keeps them 64 pt apart, none within 44 pt of the end. Zooms above the clip as 26 pt chips in the chip fill (black 0.08, white 0.16 in Dark; doubled under Increase Contrast) with a 0.5 pt separator edge (the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast) and the scale in label ink, 11 pt, monospaced digits ("2.0×"); the chosen one is ringed by a 2 pt accent line, its scale semibold, with 3 × 12 pt accent grips at its ends. The accent is never a fill behind the scale. The camera's layouts, while the recording has a camera that is not side by side, as 26 pt chips in a lane under the clip, 6 pt below it, dressed as the zoom chips are (their fill, edge, ring, and grips) and holding the layout's symbol in 11 pt label ink (`pip`, `web.camera`, `display`) and, from 120 pt wide, its name ("Camera only"); they never overlap. A cut covers its stretch in the window background at 0.85 with the scissors and how much shorter the movie is ("−0.6 s", where 64 pt allow), in secondary ink, or in the accent with a 2 pt accent ring when chosen. A stretch chosen for Cut is the accent at 0.22 with a 1.5 pt accent line. The playhead is a 2 pt accent line with a 9 pt accent dot on the ruler.

### Replay
The last 15 seconds of the displays the user chooses, kept while the replay is on, so what just happened can be saved after it happened (macOS 27; nothing of it shows on macOS 26). ScreenCaptureKit keeps the seconds itself, encoded, in its own process.

- **Status menu:** under Record Screen, Keep Last 15 Seconds opens a submenu made each time it opens: a Displays header and one item per connected display by its name, checked while that display is kept (a click starts or stops it); a separator; a Sound in Saved Clips header and four radio items, System and Microphone, System Only, Microphone Only, and No Sound, remembered in Settings (with microphone access off, the two with the microphone step back, their check kept for when access returns). Then Save Last 15 Seconds with its shortcut (none by default in the release build), enabled while a display is kept and no save is under way. No glyph of its own: the system's capture indicator says the screen is being taken, and the orange dot says the microphone is. While a recording runs the menu bar item stops it rather than opening the menu, and with the icon hidden there is no menu: the shortcut still saves.
- **On and off:** on from the moment a display is checked, until it is unchecked, its display goes away, Screen Recording is turned off, or the app quits; the next launch starts with nothing kept. Microphone access is asked before the first display starts (never once keeping has begun); without it, only the system's sound is kept. Starting or stopping announces "Keeping the last 15 seconds of [display]." or "Stopped keeping the last 15 seconds of [display]."; a display unchecked while it is still starting (its microphone question open) stops as soon as it has; one that can't start beeps and announces "Couldn’t keep the last 15 seconds of [display]."; a buffer that fails on its own beeps as it stops.
- **What is kept:** each display at its own pixels, scaled to fit 4096 on both sides (the encoder's limit: the ultrawide at 2× is kept at 4096 × 1714), at Settings' frame rate (30 in Low Power Mode), the pointer as Settings shows it, the system's sound and the microphone in tracks of their own; never the app's own windows except pins, as recordings.
- **Saving:** Save Last 15 Seconds keeps one clip per display kept, each as a stopped recording is kept (the history, the clipboard and saving as Settings › Recording › After recording say, a recording card), named for when it began and the app in front, with the sound the menu chose mixed into one track. One save at a time; a display unchecked during a save stops once the save is kept. One announcement says how it went for every display: "Saved the last 15 seconds." (or "…of 2 displays."), then for a display where nothing changed in those seconds, so there was nothing to keep, "Nothing changed on [display] in the last 15 seconds.", and "The last 15 seconds couldn’t be saved." for any other failure, with one beep when any display had nothing or failed. Saving with nothing kept beeps and announces "Nothing is being kept. Choose a display in Keep Last 15 Seconds in the menu bar." Quitting waits for clips being saved.

### Scrolling Capture
One long picture of something that scrolls: a page, a chat, a list, a wide table. Scrolling Capture opens the capture overlay (live, never frozen: what scrolls has to be seen), an area of one display is dragged, or Space and a click take a window's frame, and the overlay then frames the area as a recording's choice is framed, with the scrolling bar under it. Starting closes the overlay; the user scrolls the app under the area with their own wheel or trackpad, or lets the app scroll it, and the picture grows at whichever end is new, down or up, right or left (the first scroll that lines up decides which).

- **Choosing:** as Screen Recording's choosing: the screen dimmed 26%, the area framed with the in-place frame's corner arms, movable, resizable by its handles or dragged anew outside it, its size in pixels as a reading above its corner. Return or a double-click inside starts; Escape ends an adjustment, else cancels.
- **Scrolling bar:** the recording bar's capsule and placement (42 pt, 30 pt chips 2 pt apart, centered 12 pt under the frame, above it without room, 40 pt inside its bottom edge when neither has room): Start Capture (`arrow.up.and.down.text.horizontal`, its label in 12 pt semibold), Auto-scroll (`arrow.down.circle`, 12 pt medium), a separator, and Cancel (`xmark`). Symbols 13 pt medium. The capsule is `CapsuleBar`, the bars' shared form.
- **While capturing:** the area keeps Self-Timer's outline, nothing dimmed. Beside it a panel: a card (12 pt continuous corners, white in Light and the window background in Dark, the 0.5 pt separator hairline or the 1 pt contrast hairline under Increase Contrast, the card shadow) holding the picture so far, 156 pt across the scroll and at most 560 pt along it (less on a short display), shown where the view is (while it scrolls down the view's bottom at the preview's bottom, while it scrolls up its top at the top, so what just came into view is always in it), inset 6 pt with 6 pt corners and a 0.5 pt separator edge; under it the size in pixels in the reading face, 12 pt semibold ("1280 × 6548"); and, when one helps, a hint in 11 pt medium secondary ink, centered: "Scroll to capture" before anything has scrolled, "The views no longer line up. Let the page settle, or scroll back a little." after two views in a row that line up with nothing, "This is as long as a capture can be. Click Done to keep it." at the limit, "Scrolling…" during automatic scrolling and "Paused" when paused, "Auto-scroll needs Accessibility. Turn it on in System Settings › Privacy & Security." until scrolling by hand goes on without it, "Paused" after the user takes over from Auto-scroll, and "Auto-scroll stopped: nothing more came into view. Click Done, or scroll on by hand." once Auto-scroll has found the end (it stays until something else is said), the most pressing first in that order. Under the card, 10 pt down, the control bar in the bar's capsule: Done (`checkmark`, its label 12 pt semibold, its tooltip naming the shortcut that also ends the capture), Auto, its symbol the way it will scroll (`arrowtriangle.down.fill`, or `.up`, `.left`, `.right`, the tooltip saying it: "Scroll down automatically"), then Pause (`pause.fill`) while scrolling and Resume (the way's arrow again) while paused, in a fixed 80 pt button, a separator, Cancel (`xmark`). Once Auto-scroll starts, the card has a native speed slider below the status, labelled "Speed" with its current multiplier (0.25×–2×, initially 1×). It updates while scrolling and remains visible while paused. The panel stands 12 pt right of the area, its top at the area's top (left of it without room, inside its right edge when neither has room); for a sideways capture, 12 pt under it, at its left edge (above it, or inside its bottom edge). Above pop-up menus, on every Space, never key: the app being scrolled keeps the keyboard. The preview follows the picture at most ten times a second, and the last growth always shows.
- **Ending:** Done in the panel, the shortcut again, or Finish Scrolling Capture in the menu bar (Scrolling Capture's item while one runs, with Cancel Scrolling Capture under it); never Escape, which belongs to the app being scrolled. A stream that ends on its own (its display gone, Screen Recording turned off) beeps and keeps what was stitched. The panel is made anew for each capture, so it takes Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast as they are when it starts.
- **Auto-scroll:** needs Accessibility. Chosen on the scrolling bar without it, macOS is asked the first time (System Settings opens after that) and nothing is captured; chosen in the panel without it, only the hint shows. One click starts scrolling as soon as the first frame is captured, initially downward, or continuing the most recent manual direction. The default speed is moderate; the panel slider changes it immediately. Pause stops scrolling and Resume continues at the selected speed. The pointer moves freely and never controls speed or changes the original target window. Wheel events are addressed to that window; hardware wheel input is observed before smoothing applications transform it. Clicking or manually scrolling the target, typing in its app, or pressing Escape pauses automatic scrolling and keeps the capture open. Panel controls do not interrupt the driver. A page boundary never finishes the capture. Pushed on with nothing more coming into view (the end of the page, or a page that cannot scroll), Auto-scroll looks once more (an app busy drawing may stand still a moment and catch up) and stops by itself within about two seconds, at once when the elastic pull shows the end; it lets go of what the pull showed past the end, and says so; the capture stays open. It judges by the capture as it stands, not by the panel, which may show it a moment late. A blank stretch taller than the view shows nothing coming in either: Auto-scroll pushes on through three of the area's lengths of it before it takes it for the end (the elastic pull ends it at once). A capture falling behind the page (views slow to take in) is no end either, for up to three looks in a row, unless the pull already shows the end. Two unmatched views in a row once it has pushed an eighth of the area (views lost before the page could move, a video playing in the first view, are no reason), or the length limit, stop it too and leave the preview available for recovery or Done. Something inside the page that took the gesture and scrolls alone beside content that stays put (a code block, a map) is left out of the picture, and the gesture moves beside it so the page itself scrolls; where nothing else scrolls, it is captured after all. The way it goes follows the user's own scrolling: a quarter of the area scrolled the other way before Auto-scroll first ran, a whole area once it has, turns it; what springs back as it stops never does. The outline and panel are excluded from the capture.
- **The picture:** what stays put while the rest scrolls (a site's bar, a toolbar, a banner that pops up, a spreadsheet's frozen column) appears once, at the start as the view that reached furthest back showed it and at the end as the one that reached furthest on did. A column that never moves beside what scrolls (a forum's sidebar, a topic's timeline, a site's navigation and its buttons) appears once, as the first view showed it, with its own background color below (below a sticky card shorter than the view, the page's color around it, not the card's). One that scrolls with the page and then stays put (a sidebar held by its foot at the view's, a contents list that sticks) appears once, as it scrolled, with the page's background below it: what a view shows of it where it stopped partway through a scroll, or after it stuck, never takes the place of what the views showed while it moved, so its avatars and names show once, where they were, and the small print at its foot with them; what comes in below it later (a page's footer) is kept, what floats over it (a button fading in) is not. What changes after the first view took it (a picture that loads once scrolling starts, a skeleton filled in) comes out as it ended up. A block of the page that keeps changing where it is (a video, an animated advertisement, a carousel) comes out as one frame, taken when the whole of it was in view, rather than each line from another view; one never shown whole (taller than the view) still comes out line by line, as before. A stretch of blank page taller than the view, which no view can measure, comes out as long as a view showed it, with what follows after it rather than lost or lined up with something alike before it (a shop's grid of cards alike); never past something a view showed in between that could not be placed. Views of nothing but the stretch, of the last few lines of what came before leaving above it, or of the stretch and a line or two of what follows, are taken as nothing scrolled, not as lost, so the panel does not ask to scroll back while they go by and Auto-scroll goes on through it. A view further on than one view reaches (an anchor followed, a key that jumps), or showing something else altogether, lines up with nothing, rather than with something the page repeats (a phrase, a reply link) somewhere else, wherever most of what it shows says otherwise. A first scroll further than a banner above a header is tall follows the page, not the header and sidebars, which move only the banner's height and stick. Pushed past the page's end, the browser pulls the whole view along, header and sidebars too, then springs back: none of it comes into the picture, neither the background the pull showed past the end nor a header or sidebar where the pull moved them, beside a sidebar's rule or a button fixed on screen as on a plain page. What sticks over part of the page for a while (a post's avatar stuck below the header while the post scrolls, a chat button in a corner) is left where the page has it: each spot of the picture holds what the views showed there moving with the page the longest, so an avatar shows once, where its post begins, and the page shows under the button. A button fixed anywhere over the page (a back-to-top button, a share or Post button, the highlighted item of a sidebar that stays put) is left out the same way, its flat inside and its edges with its mark, rather than stamped down the picture wherever a line passed under it nearer the view's middle than any view showed the line again; at the picture's end, lines seen only under it show the page's background there, where it sat over the page's margin. An overlay scroll bar's knob, over each line in a view or two, is left out: each line keeps its ends as most views showed them, and views taken while nothing scrolls count most, where a line between the ends shows nothing did (over a sheet's empty cells a view that scrolled looks still, and its row numbers are other rows'); where what the first view showed of a line gave way to something else altogether (a picture loaded since), its ends follow. A contents list that sticks by the view's edge appears whole, its short lines and the blank after them to the edge staying put with it, though no line of it reaches far down the view. A window chosen with Space (or an area whose corners are a window's) comes out with square corners: past the window's rounded corners shows the desktop, still while the page scrolls, so in each view what lies past them takes what the same column shows just inside, and nothing of the desktop is left down the picture where the first view ended. Drawn in Display P3. At most 50,000 pixels long or 1 GB (a capture as wide as a Retina display's window reaches about 47,500 pixels, some twenty screens); at the limit it stops growing and says so. Kept as a screenshot is (the history, the clipboard and saving as Settings say, the shutter, a card), but never framed by the automatic background (a frame around a picture this long would take gigabytes); a card shows the top of a picture more than 2.5 times taller than wide, and the left end of one more than 5 times wider than tall, rather than a thin strip, made from the picture at its own size so it stays sharp however long the picture is.
- **VoiceOver:** the framed area reads as "Scrolling capture area" with its size, its help "Press Return to start, then scroll what is under the area. Press Escape to cancel."; the bars read as "Scrolling Capture", Auto/Pause/Resume as buttons with their current action, speed as a labelled slider; the card as "Captured so far" with the size. Starting announces "Scroll what is under the area to capture it. Click Done, or press [the shortcut's keys] again, when you have all of it."; losing track, the limit, Accessibility being off, and Auto-scroll handing back are announced as their hints.

### Capture History Search
Captures are found by the words in them. Typed into the History toolbar's search field, words narrow the grid, within the Show filter, to the captures whose text holds every word, each in the text or in the name of the app the capture was taken in (so a recording is found by its app). As OCR spaces and breaks text one way on one line and another on the next, what is compared leaves out spaces, line breaks, and punctuation, and counts case, full and half width, Latin accents, and Traditional against Simplified Chinese as the same: "电脑设定" finds "電腦 設定" read over a line break, "软件更新" finds "软件 › 更新", "cafe" finds "Café"; in kana the voicing marks stay, so か does not find が. Words are matched as typed, not by meaning or spelling.

- **The card while searching:** in place of "W × H · App", the line of the capture's text where the first word typed was found: up to 18 columns before it, a Chinese, Japanese, or Korean character taking two, so what was found starts on the first line in any language ("…" first when the line goes on), what was found in label ink and semibold, and the rest of the line, in caption secondary on at most two lines. A capture found by its app alone keeps "W × H · App". VoiceOver reads the card's time and the same line, without the "…".
- **Reading the text:** every screenshot's text is read once, by the same reading as Capture Text, and kept beside it in the history, so a search is instant. A capture is read a few seconds after it is taken, at background priority (captures taken one after another are read together), and again when its picture changes (marked up, a redaction among the marks, or put back as taken): what is searched is what the picture shows. A large picture is read in overlapping bands across its width (a very wide one also cut across), so its text is read at the size it was captured, a scrolling capture all the way down. Not read: recordings, captures with history off, and, in Low Power Mode, anything until Capture History opens, which reads whatever is waiting at once (a reading already going in the background goes on at once, at the higher priority, keeping what it read). Each run reads in a process of its own, the text reader inside the app, which exits when nothing is left: what Vision uses to read is never kept by the menu bar app. The text never leaves the Mac and is removed with its capture.
- **States:** while captures are still being read, below what was found, a small spinner and "Still reading the text in N captures." in callout secondary with steady digits, centered, one element for VoiceOver, so a search that finds less than expected says why. Nothing found: the empty state with a magnifying glass, "No Results for “words”", and either "Still reading the text in N captures." or "Captures are found by the words in them and the app they were taken in." Found only in what the Show filter leaves out: "No Screenshots Found" (or "No Recordings Found"), "Found in N captures under All.", and a Show All button. Once typing pauses, VoiceOver hears how many captures were found, or "No Results". Clearing the field shows the whole history again, as it was.
- **Narrow windows:** at the window's 560 pt minimum the search field keeps its room and the leading controls (filter, Keep for, Move All to Trash) go into the toolbar's overflow menu, as the system lays it out.

### Printing
A screenshot prints through the system print panel, with its preview, copies, page range, paper size, orientation, and scaling. Print… is in a screenshot's Quick Access card menu, in Capture History (its context menu, and ⌘P for the selected screenshot), in a picture pin's menu (⌘P), and in the editor's File menu (⌘P), where it prints the capture as it looks, marks and background and all. Recordings, text pins, and color pins have nothing to print. The panel comes down as a sheet on the History window or the editor; from a card or a pin it opens on its own, the app brought forward.

- **Size:** the picture prints at the size it was on screen (its pixels over its display's pixels per point), centered, and is shrunk only when it is larger than the page, never enlarged. The page is turned whichever way prints it larger (as the picture is shaped when it fits either way), so a screen or a window of any common shape prints on one page.
- **Long captures:** a picture runs onto more pages only when that prints it at least 1.5 times as large as one page would: a scrolling capture is fitted to an upright page's width and runs on down as many pages as it takes. Each page ends in the nearest gap above where it would end, within the last fifth of the page, so no line is cut in half: three points running where the picture repeats the row above (between lines of text, under a picture), a sidebar, scrollbar, or border beside it included; with no gap there, it ends where it would. On real scrolling captures nearly every page break finds one. A picture as much wider than a page is fitted to a landscape page's height and runs across.
- **Job name:** the capture's file name ("Pin" for a pin, the window's title in the editor), as the print queue shows it.

### Saved Files
A screenshot is kept in Capture History as a lossless PNG of the capture as it was taken, whatever Settings say: marking up, reading its text, redaction suggestions, and restoring depend on it. Where a file leaves the app it is written as Settings › General says (Format, Pictures, File name, Clipboard): a copy saved to the export folder (Save on a card, in History, in the editor, or on a pin, and saving after a capture), Save As…, a drag out of a card, History, or the editor, an upload, a Shortcuts result, and the picture on the clipboard, which stays PNG. Recordings are never changed. One routine does it all (`PictureExport`), so every way out writes the same picture.

- **Format:** PNG (the default), JPEG, or HEIC, in a picker with a 110 pt quality slider beside it (50 to 100 percent in 5 percent steps, 90 by default, dimmed while PNG is chosen) and its reading to the right in secondary ink with monospaced digits, 40 pt wide; the slider reads as its percentage for VoiceOver. A capture left as a PNG with nothing else asked for is copied as it is, undecoded, so the defaults write the bytes the history holds. JPEG has no transparency: a window's clear corners and shadow are flattened on white. PNG and HEIC keep them.
- **Pictures:** three checkboxes, all off, each with a help button. Save Retina screenshots at 1× scales a picture taken at 2× or more to its size in points (the scale is recorded with each capture; a capture from before that, or a file with no resolution, is left at its size). Convert to sRGB draws a Display P3 picture in sRGB, not just labelled so; one in sRGB already is left alone. Add a 1 px border darkens the outermost ring of pixels of the picture as written (the size stays), black at 20%, each corner once; a picture with any transparent pixel on its edge (a window with its shadow, the rounded corners of one without) gets none. The three are done together in one pass, scaled first. A picture written other than as it was kept states its resolution: 144 dpi at 2×, 72 at 1×.
- **HDR:** a HEIC of a capture that has an extended-range copy is written with an ISO HDR gain map, the SDR picture ImageIO makes of it as the base (`kCGImageDestinationEncodeToISOGainmap`). The copy is asked of ScreenCaptureKit only when HEIC is chosen, Convert to sRGB is off, and the display can show more than SDR, and only by captures taken live (an area with Freeze the screen off, Self-Timer, Capture Previous Area, fullscreen), not from the frozen screen, which is the default, nor a window, a scrolling capture, or an area across displays. The Format help says so ("With Freeze the screen and Convert to sRGB off…"). It goes with the picture as captured, scaled and bordered with it: marks or a background (in-place annotation, the automatic background) drop it. It is there for the file written as the capture is delivered (saving after a capture, in-place Save) and for the clipboard's file; a copy saved later from a card or History is an SDR HEIC. Convert to sRGB gives up the gain map.
- **File name:** a pattern in a 250 pt rounded text field (prompt: the standard pattern, "OhMyShot {date} at {time}"), a help button naming the tokens, below it a row of five small bordered buttons (date, time, app, window, counter: each token's name without its braces, in the caption size monospaced, at its own width so all five fit; tooltip "Insert {date}" and so on) that each add their token to the end of the pattern, and below those, in callout secondary ink and middle-truncated at 300 pt, "Next: " and the name the next file would take with its extension, read again every second (it is made up with the app Safari and the window Welcome). `{date}` is 2026-10-07, `{time}` 22.45.10, `{app}` the app in front when the capture began, `{window}` the captured window's title or, for an area or a screen, the title of the front window of the app in front (the first standard window in the window list that has one; empty when none, and only looked up when the pattern has the token), `{counter}` a number from 1 that goes up by one with each file saved. A title is cut to 80 characters, a whole name to 200 bytes of UTF-8 (room for " (12)" and the extension); slashes become hyphens, colons periods, control characters and the dots that would hide the file go. An empty result is "OhMyShot". A name already taken in the folder gets " (2)", " (3)".
  - **When a name is made:** a capture is named when it is taken (that is its file in History, which a drag-out and an upload show), with the number it would take, which it does not take yet. A copy saved from it is named again when it is saved, from the pattern as it is then and the capture's own date, app, and window, and takes the next number then; so the counter counts files saved, not captures, and saving the same capture twice makes two numbers. A number is taken only when the pattern has `{counter}`, and at the moment the name is offered, before any alert or panel waits for the person, so copies named together (a name being asked for on one card while another is saved, a Save As panel left open) never get the same number. It goes back if the copy is not saved under the name offered: the alert or panel is cancelled, or the name typed in Ask for a name or Save As is the person's own; only the latest number can go back, so when another copy took one meanwhile the gap stays. A picture that came with a name (one opened from a file) keeps it. A recording takes its number when it is made, being the one file it will ever be, and its saved copy keeps the name.
- **Ask for a name:** a checkbox under the file name, off by default. Save and saving after a capture first show a small alert, "Name the file", with "It is saved in “Desktop” as a .png file." (the folder by its Finder name), a 320 pt text field holding the name the pattern gives, selected, and Save (Return) and Cancel. Escape or Cancel saves nothing: the capture stays on its card, in History, and on the clipboard, the counter does not move, and nothing beeps. What is typed loses a picture extension it ends with, is made a valid name, and an empty one is the offered name. All-In-One's Save button and ⌘S ask like any Save, as does saving after a capture taken at the keyboard. A save the URL API asked for (`action=save`, or any command's action, Save after capture included) or a shortcut asked for never asks, being nobody's to answer, nor does Quick Access's Save All or a card's Save and Close; recordings are not asked. The alert starts from the run loop, not inside a task, which a modal would hold. Save As… opens the system panel in the export folder with the same name and the extension of the format; the panel allows the other two formats, and the one typed in the name is the one written (a screenshot saved as HEIC can be saved as `.png` by naming it so). A file it replaces stays as it was until the new one is written whole.
- **Clipboard:** Picture only (the default) puts PNG (and TIFF for older apps, made when asked for) and no file, as it always has; some apps paste a file where the picture was wanted. File and picture adds the file beside the picture: the capture's own file in History when nothing else is asked for and History keeps captures (with Keep captures on Never the file would go when the card closes), else a converted copy in the caches named as the capture is (in `Clipboard`, which keeps the last five, so copying again and again does not fill the caches), so Finder, Mail, and chat apps paste the file while image editors paste the picture. Both follow 1×, sRGB, and the border for the picture. With the file beside it the clipboard is written once the capture is kept (the picture alone if keeping fails). A pin's Copy is the picture alone; the editor's Copy follows the settings.
- **Handing over:** a drag out of a card, History, or the editor hands over the capture's own file when nothing is asked for, else a converted copy named as the capture, made as the drag starts and kept (`~/Library/Caches/<bundle id>/Exports`, a folder per way of writing it, removed after three days: at launch, then about hourly while copies are made, with the folders they leave empty), so a second drag, a retried upload, and a clipboard file are quick. An upload sends the same copy under that name; the capture is read when the upload is asked for, so with History off it need not stay on until the copy is made. A Shortcuts result is the picture as written, named so, read at once. Share… on a card and in History hands over the same copy, made when it is chosen (History's is a button that opens the system share sheet where the pointer is, not a `ShareLink`, which would make every capture's copy as the menu opens); Open, Show in Finder, Quick Look, and printing use the capture's own file.
- **VoiceOver:** the picker reads "Format", the slider "Quality" with its percentage, the token buttons "Insert {date}" and so on, the pattern field "File name", the preview "Next: …"; the alert's field reads "File name".

### Share Extension
OhMyShot is in every app's Share menu (and Finder's) as a sharing extension named "OhMyShot" ("OhMyShot Dev" in the Debug app), with the app's icon. It appears only for pictures: images shared from Preview, Safari, Photos, or Finder (the app takes the first 20 of a larger share). macOS keeps a new sharing extension off until the person turns it on, as it does every app's: in the Share menu's Edit Extensions… or System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions › Sharing, where it is switched off again too.

- **The sheet:** a 340 × 120 pt panel, 20 pt side and 18 pt top and bottom padding. "Send to OhMyShot" in 13 pt semibold; under it, 6 pt below, "1 picture to mark up in the editor or keep on screen." in 12 pt secondary ink (the count agrees with its noun, "2 pictures"; only pictures are counted, so a PDF or a folder shared with them is left out and not handed over); 14 pt below that, the buttons at the trailing edge, 8 pt apart: Cancel (Escape), Pin, and Annotate (the default, Return). Annotate opens each picture in the annotation editor, added to Capture History first as any other picture opened there is; Pin puts each on the screen as a picture pin. Neither saves, copies, or shows a Quick Access card.
- **Busy and failed:** after a choice the buttons dim and a small spinner shows beside them while the pictures are copied ("Sending…" in place of the line). A share with no picture says "There is no picture here. OhMyShot takes pictures to mark up or pin." and a failed hand-over "The pictures could not be handed to OhMyShot. Open OhMyShot once, then try again."; both leave only Close (Return). A hand-over fails when the pictures cannot be copied and also when the system cannot open the app; the sheet says so, with the copies removed, instead of closing as if the share had worked. Pin and Annotate stay dim for the instant it takes to tell which items are pictures (a file Finder shares by its address alone is told by its name). One picture that cannot be read does not stop the others.
- **Hand-over:** the extension is sandboxed and the app is not, so it copies the pictures into a folder named by a UUID in the app's own Application Support (`Share Inbox`, its one writable place, a sandbox exception) and opens the app with `ohmyshot://share?id=<folder>&action=annotate` (`pin`; `ohmyshot-dev` for the Debug app). The app reads and opens the pictures one at a time, so a large share does not hold every one decoded at once, then removes the folder; folders nobody collected go after a day. The URL names a folder, never a path, and the app takes from it only regular files directly in that folder that ImageIO reads as pictures (no links, at most 20, none over 512 MB). The command skips the Automation switch and the approval prompt: choosing OhMyShot in a Share menu is the approval, and it can reach nothing but that folder.
- **VoiceOver:** the title and the line are read as they stand, each button by its title; "Sending…" and a failure are announced as they appear, as VoiceOver does not follow text that changes by itself.

### Open With
Pictures open in OhMyShot from Finder's Open With menu and from other apps' Open With, and OhMyShot can be made the app a type opens with (Get Info › Open with › Change All…). It declares every image type (`public.image`) with the Editor role at Alternate rank, so it is offered but never takes a type from the app that has it, and it owns its project files (`.ohmyshot`). A picture opened this way goes to the annotation editor, added to Capture History first as Annotate Image… adds it, named after the file and at the resolution the file carries (144 dpi is 2 pixels per point; a file with none, or 72 dpi, is 1, and is kept in Capture History at 1, so saving at 1× leaves it at its size rather than halve it on a guess, and a pin or a print shows it at its size); a picture sent to the editor from a Share menu, by Open from Clipboard, or by Shortcuts' Annotate Image is taken the same way (a picture pinned from a Share menu is shown at the scale of the display the pointer is on, as one pinned from the clipboard is); one editor opens for each picture, and a project opens in its own. A picture that cannot be read beeps and is logged; a file that is not a picture is passed over. Files opened while the app is still starting wait until it has started.

### Motion
- Cards slide in 36 pt from the screen edge of their corner (the left edge, or the right with the stack at the bottom-right) with a fade over 0.22 s ease-out; the stack reflows over 0.22 s ease-out; a dismissed card slides 40 pt toward that edge and fades. Hover fades take 0.12 s ease-out. History scrolls to a new selection over 0.15 s ease-out.
- The text selection highlight fades out over 0.35 s once the text is copied (0.15 s when there was none).
- The optional text result card fades in over 0.22 s with a 6 pt rise of its layer (never of its window, whose size can change mid-animation), and fades out over 0.12 s.
- In the OCR panel, Copy buttons swap their symbol for a checkmark with the system's symbol replace transition and back after 1.5 s (toolbar) or 1.2 s (rows).
- The translation lens fades in over 0.18 s ease-out and out over 0.15 s ease-in; its buttons fade over 0.12 s.
- In the annotation editor, the style capsule fades in over 0.12 s ease-out when a new selection or tool calls for it and simply moves when the picture zooms or scrolls; it hides while a mark is drawn, moved, or reshaped. In in-place annotation the capsule fades in the same way (0.12 s ease-out) for a new tool or selection and hides while a mark is drawn or the selection is adjusted; the toolbar and selection chrome appear and move without animation. Canvas layers never animate on their own, so marks, handles, and the crop frame stay under the pointer.
- Redaction suggestion outlines fade in over 0.12 s ease-out when a reading finishes; picking up the Redact tool again, covering one, or ending a hand-drawn redaction shows or removes them at once.
- The Self-Timer countdown's outline and disc fade in together over 0.12 s ease-out; its ring runs out linearly over the whole delay while the digit steps down each second; at zero it is gone at once, with no fade out.
- A pin's reading shows at once and, after 0.9 s, fades over 0.12 s; its Close and More fade over 0.12 s on hover. A color pin's reading gives way back to its hex at once instead of fading. The pin itself moves, zooms, and fades its picture, card, or swatch without animation, so it stays under the pointer.
- The upload badge fades in over 0.12 s; its ring steps and never eases, and a finished badge goes after 3 s.
- The Copied reading shows at once and, after 0.9 s, fades over 0.12 s. The ruler's "Copied" stays 0.9 s and gives way to the length at once; its box goes from 0.6 to solid opacity at once.
- The color card fades in over 0.22 s with a 6 pt rise of its layer, as the text result card does, and fades out over 0.12 s; its copy chips swap to a checkmark and back after 1.2 s, without a transition.
- Recording's control bar fades in over 0.12 s; the keystrokes fade in and out over 0.12 s; a click ring widens and fades over 0.4 s, ease-out (a press's own feedback, as the system's circle is); the camera bubble goes to filling what is recorded and back through a 0.12 s fade out and in. The recording bar's chips change at once.
- In a Studio movie, a zoom eases in and out over up to 0.5 s (a third of it when shorter, cubic ease-in-out), the camera glides after the cursor through a spring (0.35 s) and, fully zoomed, only when the cursor nears the edge of what it shows; click rings and captions move as recording draws them. These are the movie's own motion, drawn into it for its viewers, and are kept under Reduce Motion. The Studio window itself animates nothing: its timeline, inspector, and preview change at once.
- Under Reduce Motion all of these are instant (a pin's reading and the Copied reading simply go after 0.9 s); the Self-Timer countdown appears without a fade and its ring steps down each second instead of running out. Nothing trails the pointer: overlay layers update without animation. The two durations live in `Theme.Motion` (`slide` 0.22 s, `fade` 0.12 s).

### Future Surfaces
Studio's speed changes, redaction in the movie, a GIF from Studio, and a zoomable timeline for long recordings are not built. They must use this system (system colors and accent, 12 pt cards and capsules, soft lift shadows, rounded readings off-window, small glass only, the three accessibility responses); their specs are recorded when they ship, not before.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** use system semantic colors and `controlAccentColor` for every surface and state; add only black or white at the stated opacities.
- **Do** make every floating control a full capsule (radius = height / 2) with concentric inner chips, and every picture a 12 pt continuous-corner card; the exceptions are a picture being edited for export, which shows its own square edges, and a pin, whose corners are the user's (0 to 24 pt, 12 by default).
- **Do** give floating elements a soft downward shadow plus a 0.5 pt hairline so they hold on white pages.
- **Do** put live numbers in a reading capsule: SF Pro Rounded 12 pt semibold with monospaced digits.
- **Do** keep text-bearing controls opaque over captures; use glass for the rim, not the fill.
- **Do** answer accessibility settings specifically: Reduce Motion = instant; Reduce Transparency = solid window-colored capsule; Increase Contrast = 45% dimming, 0.7 outline, 1 pt contrast hairlines and button outlines, doubled chip fills, 2 pt ruler lines over a 4 pt solid white outline, 2 pt suggestion dashes over a 2 pt solid white line.
- **Do** use standard windows, toolbars, and SwiftUI glass button styles inside windows; Settings panes use the two-column layout, other windows' forms are grouped.
- **Do** name every control VoiceOver can reach, a symbol alone included: a toolbar's split Save button takes a picture described "Save" (`Theme.symbol`), since VoiceOver names that segment by its picture and a symbol given by name reads as its English name; its menu segment stays "menu button", which SwiftUI gives no way to name.
- **Do** build chips in floating controls (the All-In-One bar, the in-place toolbar, the tool rail, the style capsule) as borderless buttons with the chip fills, and hand-draw whole controls only where AppKit would render them dimmed (non-key floating panels), tracking presses like standard buttons.
- **Do** regenerate the app icon, the menu bar glyph, and the wordmark only from their generators (`Tools/logo/make_icon.py`, `Tools/logo/make_wordmark.py`); never hand-edit `App/AppIcon.icon` or the MenuBarIcon SVG.

### Don't:
- **Don't** bring back Contact Sheet: no frame numbers, no captures joined into a strip, no film-edge or darkroom motifs.
- **Don't** build dark heavy panels, bars, or strips; small dark round corner buttons over a hovered picture or pin, and a locked pin's mark in the same style, are the only dark chrome.
- **Don't** put glass, blur, or a material over the full-screen overlay; the room is a flat 26% fill.
- **Don't** use hard offset shadows, glows, or halos; a thin overlay line's contrast outline (dark under white, white under the ruler's accent and a suggestion's dashes) is not one.
- **Don't** use the rounded face inside standard windows, or proportional digits in a changing number.
- **Don't** invent a brand hue or tint chrome toward one; fixed hues belong to mark inks, background fills, and the brand mark only.
- **Don't** animate anything that follows the pointer, or run any motion under Reduce Motion.
- **Don't** fill an outline around text the user is judging, or let its contrast line reach onto the glyphs; outlines that sit tight around text take a white line no wider than their own.
- **Don't** give the editor a wide tool toolbar or a fixed option strip; tools live in the rail and a mark's style beside the mark.
- **Don't** bring back viewfinder brackets, camera bodies, or scissors as the mark; the mark is the loupe.
